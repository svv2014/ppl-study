# PPL Study Curriculum Plan

**Exam:** Transport Canada PPL (Aeroplane) Written Exam  
**Target score:** 80%+ (minimum passing: 60%)  
**Format:** ~100 multiple-choice questions, 3.5 hours  
**Session length:** 20 minutes per lesson  
**Last updated:** 2026-04-16

---

## Exam Topic Weights (approximate)

| Section | Exam Weight | Difficulty | Lessons Planned |
|---------|-------------|------------|-----------------|
| Air Law | ~30–35% | Medium (rule memorization) | 18 |
| Navigation | ~25–30% | High (calculation + charts) | 14 |
| Meteorology | ~20–25% | Medium-High (concepts + decode) | 14 |
| General Knowledge | ~15–20% | Medium (systems + math) | 14 |
| **Total** | **100%** | — | **60 lessons** |

At one lesson per day (20 min), the full curriculum takes approximately **60 days (8–9 weeks)**.

---

## Recommended Study Order

**Start with Air Law → Navigation → Meteorology → General Knowledge**

**Why Air Law first:**
1. Highest exam weight (~30–35%) — most questions, most return on study time
2. Rule-based content — facts and numbers that reward early memorization and spaced repetition
3. Required for PSTAR (the prerequisite exam) — material overlaps and reinforces prior knowledge
4. Airspace concepts are referenced constantly across all other sections

**Why Navigation second:**
- Chart reading and dead reckoning are required for cross-country planning questions that blend with Met and Air Law
- Calculation skills (wind correction, time-speed-distance) need time to become automatic

**Why Meteorology third:**
- Conceptual framework is rich — needs time to absorb before it clicks
- METAR/TAF decoding is mechanical; best learned after Nav chart work creates study momentum

**Why General Knowledge last:**
- Lowest exam weight, but still important
- Systems and performance topics are most natural to learn near the end when the full flight picture is assembled

## Recommended Daily Study Schedule

### Phase 1 — Weeks 1–3: Air Law (18 lessons)
Focus on memorization and pattern recognition. Use flashcards for airspace class requirements and weather minimums.

### Phase 2 — Weeks 4–5: Navigation (14 lessons)
Bring a plotter and E6B computer. Practice calculations alongside each lesson.

### Phase 3 — Weeks 6–7: Meteorology (14 lessons)
Look up a real METAR and TAF after Lesson MET-005 and MET-006. Decoding practice accelerates retention.

### Phase 4 — Week 8: General Knowledge (14 lessons)
Work through a Pilot's Operating Handbook alongside these lessons if one is available.

### Phase 5 — Week 9: Review and Practice Exams
- Use TP 13014 (Transport Canada sample questions) daily
- Take timed practice exams on PilotExamCanada.com
- Target 85%+ on practice exams before booking the real exam

## Web App Vision

The PPL study curriculum is designed as the content layer for a listen-along study app — modelled on the audio-content format at suprun.ca. Each lesson file doubles as both a reading script and the source for a generated audio track; a learner opens the app, picks a topic and lesson number, hits play, and follows along with narration while key visuals (chart excerpts, tables) display on screen. After the audio ends, the practice questions render as an interactive quiz with instant explanations. The architecture mirrors a podcast player layered with a flashcard deck — browse by section, track progress, replay any lesson, and share individual lessons via a clean URL.
