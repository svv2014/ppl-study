NAV-005 — Wind Correction Angle and Ground Speed

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Ch.9 · PPL Written Exam

Wind Triangle — TT 090°, Wind from 270° (30 kt), TAS 120 kt
Key Concepts

Wind Correction Angle (WCA)

TH = TT + WCA
Apply WCA to True Track to get True Heading

WCA is the angle between your intended track and the direction you must point the aircraft nose to stay on track. You crab into the wind: if wind is from the left, WCA is positive (turn right); wind from the right, turn left.

Example: TT 090°, wind from left, WCA = +14°
TH = 090° + 14° = 104°

Ground Speed (GS)

GS ≠ TAS
Wind changes your speed over the ground

Headwind component reduces GS below TAS.
Tailwind component increases GS above TAS.
Pure crosswind has only a small effect on GS (reduces it slightly).

Example: TAS 120 kt, 30 kt direct tailwind
GS ≈ 150 kt
30 kt direct headwind → GS ≈ 90 kt

Wind Effects on WCA and Ground Speed
Wind Condition WCA Direction GS vs TAS Note
Direct Headwind Zero (no crab) GS < TAS Maximum time en route; fly heading = track
Direct Tailwind Zero (no crab) GS > TAS Minimum time en route; fly heading = track
Left Crosswind (wind from left) Crab left → TH < TT GS slightly < TAS WCA is negative; subtract from TT
Right Crosswind (wind from right) Crab right → TH > TT GS slightly < TAS WCA is positive; add to TT
Memory Hooks

Key Concepts to Remember

CRAB Crab into the wind to maintain your track. Wind from the left → point nose left; wind from the right → point nose right. The track stays straight, but the heading differs.
CR-3 On the exam, use the CR-3 (or E6-B) flight computer to solve WCA and GS exactly. The wind triangle formula is: TAS vector + Wind vector = GS vector along track.
GS RULE Only headwind/tailwind components affect GS significantly. A 90° crosswind does NOT change GS by the full wind speed — the effect is small (GS ≈ √(TAS²−crosswind²)).
