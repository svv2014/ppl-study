NAV-013 — Density Altitude

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Ch.9, Ch.10 · PPL Written Exam

Density Altitude Formula

Calculating Density Altitude

DA = PA + 120 × (OAT − ISA Temp)

Where PA = Pressure Altitude (set altimeter to 29.92" Hg and read), OAT = Outside Air Temperature (°C), ISA Temp = 15°C − (2°C × altitude in thousands of ft).

Airport elevation:3,000 ft ASL
QNH:29.72" Hg (low pressure)
Pressure Altitude:3,000 + (29.92−29.72) × 1,000 = 3,200 ft PA
OAT:+30°C (hot summer day)
ISA Temp at 3,200 ft:15 − (2 × 3.2) = 15 − 6.4 = 8.6°C
Temperature deviation:30 − 8.6 = +21.4°C above ISA
Density Altitude:3,200 + (120 × 21.4) = 3,200 + 2,568 ≈ 5,768 ft DA
Nearly 6,000 ft DA at a 3,000 ft airport!
Four Factors That Raise Density Altitude
🌡️

High Temperature

Hot air is less dense. Each 1°C above ISA raises DA by ~120 ft. Summer afternoons at southern airports are prime DA risk.

↑ DA by 120 ft per °C above ISA
⛰️

High Elevation

Higher airports have less air above them — lower air density. Mountain airports combine elevation + temperature for extreme DA.

↑ DA = airport elevation (baseline)
📉

Low Atmospheric Pressure

Low QNH = higher pressure altitude for the same elevation. Frontal troughs can add hundreds of feet to effective DA.

↑ DA when QNH < 29.92" Hg
💧

High Humidity

Humid air is slightly less dense than dry air (water vapour is lighter than N₂/O₂). Effect is smaller than temp/pressure but additive.

↑ DA (modest effect vs temp)

Exam Memory Hooks

HHHIHigh, Hot, Humid = High Density Altitude = poor performance. The three Hs that kill aircraft performance. Add "Low pressure" for the full picture: H³L.
FORMULADA = PA + 120 × (OAT − ISA). Or use the E6B density altitude scale, or POH density altitude chart. All give the same result — use whichever the exam question provides data for.
POH FIRSTAlways check the POH takeoff performance chart before flight. Calculate DA for departure. If takeoff distance exceeds available runway, do not depart — reduce weight or wait for cooler conditions.
