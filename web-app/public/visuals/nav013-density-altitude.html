NAV-013 — Density Altitude

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Ch.9 · PPL Written Exam
Density Altitude Chart
Core Concepts

What is Density Altitude?

  • Pressure altitude corrected for non-standard temperature
  • Represents the air density — how "thick" the air actually is
  • High temperature = lower air density = higher density altitude
  • High elevation = lower pressure = higher density altitude
  • Humidity also reduces density slightly (moist air is less dense)
  • DA tells you what altitude the aircraft "thinks" it is flying at

Why Density Altitude Matters

  • Engine produces less power — less oxygen per intake stroke
  • Wings produce less lift — thinner air, less molecules over airfoil
  • Longer takeoff roll — both effects combined, plus higher groundspeed at rotation
  • Slower initial climb rate — critical near terrain
  • Propeller less efficient — reduced thrust
  • Never skip DA calculation at high-elevation or hot-day airports

Density Altitude Formula
DA ≈ PA + (OAT − ISA Temp at PA) × 120 ft/°C

Worked Example

  • Airport elevation = 3,500 ft → Pressure Altitude ≈ 3,500 ft (assuming QNH near standard)
  • ISA temperature at 3,500 ft = 15 − (2 × 3.5) = 8°C
  • Actual OAT = +30°C
  • Temperature excess above ISA = 30 − 8 = 22°C above ISA
  • DA ≈ 3,500 + (22 × 120) = 3,500 + 2,640 = ~6,140 ft
  • Aircraft performs as if at 6,140 ft on a standard day — significantly degraded

Memory Hook
Memory Hook
  • Hot + High = High DA = Hazard: Both high temperature and high elevation push DA up. Together they can double the apparent altitude.
  • ISA lapse rate = 2°C per 1,000 ft: At 3,000 ft, ISA = 15 − 6 = 9°C. Memorize this: start at 15°C at sea level, subtract 2°C for every 1,000 ft.
  • 120 ft per °C: Each 1°C above ISA adds 120 ft to your density altitude. On a 10°C-above-ISA day, add 1,200 ft.
  • Check POH performance charts: Always calculate DA before takeoff and compare to your aircraft's POH climb/takeoff charts — especially on short runways or near terrain.
