Landmark Types — Best to Worst
🏙️

Urban Areas / Towns

Shape visible from altitude. Road network distinctive. Best when city name readable on chart matches unique shape.

Excellent
💧

Lakes & Rivers

Distinctive shape on chart. Shoreline bends are unique. River junctions (confluences) are ideal. Avoid rivers running parallel to track.

Excellent
🛤️

Railways

Railway crossing track at right angles = precise fix. Distinctive curves and junctions. Less visible at high altitude.

Good
🛣️

Highways

Major highways visible from altitude. Interchanges distinctive. Can be confused with parallel routes — check chart carefully.

Good
⛰️

Prominent hilltops, ridges, valleys. Best in varied terrain. Verify against elevation contours on VNC. Less useful in flat areas.

Good

No distinguishing features. High risk of disorientation if only checkpoint available. Supplement with VOR or GPS.

Avoid as primary
The 1-in-60 Rule — Track Error Correction

Track Error Angle (TEA)

TEA° ≈ Track Error (NM) ÷ Distance Flown (NM) × 60

If you are 2 NM off track after flying 20 NM, your track error angle ≈ 2 ÷ 20 × 60 = 6°. To regain track by the destination (40 NM remaining), double-correct by 12° initially, then reduce correction by 6° once back on track.

The rule comes from geometry: at 60 NM from a point, 1° of bearing difference = 1 NM of displacement.

Chart-to-Ground Matching Process
1
Orient the chart — track up (chart oriented so your direction of flight is away from you). Compare chart to view ahead.
2
Identify the next checkpoint — know what you're looking for before you get there. Expected ETA from PLOG.
3
Match shape, not just position — a lake's outline, a river's bend, a town's road network. One feature could be anything; two or three confirming features give a fix.
4
Log actual time over checkpoint — compare to ETA. Time difference indicates ground speed change (wind changed?) or track error.
5
Correct if necessary — if late at checkpoint, you may have a headwind component. If early, a tailwind. Revise remaining ETAs accordingly.

Exam Memory Hooks

PERPENDICULARA river or road crossing your track at right angles gives a precise fix. Parallel features give no position information across track — only along track.
TWO-FEATURE RULENever confirm a fix from a single feature. Use at least two independent features (e.g., lake shape + road junction) to positively identify your position.
LOST EARLYIf unsure of position, maintain heading, altitude, and time. Continue DR until you can get a fix or reach a definite feature. Do not wander — wandering makes it worse.
