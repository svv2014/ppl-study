NAV-009 — Pilotage — Navigating by Ground Reference

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Ch.9 · PPL Written Exam
VFR Chart Pilotage — Visual Reference Diagram
Core Concepts

What is Pilotage?

  • Navigation by identifying visual ground references and matching them to chart features
  • Primary VFR nav method — no instruments required beyond chart and compass
  • Pilot compares what they see out the window to the sectional/VNC chart
  • Most reliable when planned checkpoints are used systematically
  • Used in combination with dead reckoning (DR) for cross-country

Checkpoint Selection Criteria

  • Prominent — easily visible from altitude
  • Unique — not easily confused with similar features
  • Distinct shape on the chart (large lake, highway X)
  • Spacing: 20–30 NM apart (about every 15–20 min)
  • Visible from well ahead — no sudden recognition required
  • Avoid features that change seasonally (e.g. dry riverbeds)

Memory Hook
Memory Hook
  • 5 NM lookout rule: Scan ahead 5 NM at all times — identify your next checkpoint before reaching the current one.
  • 1-in-60 rule hint: At 120 kt, 1° of track error = 1 NM off after 60 NM — small errors compound quickly without checkpoints.
  • 30-second rule: Never look at the chart for more than 30 seconds without scanning outside for traffic and terrain.
  • Checkpoint mnemonic — PUVS: Prominent · Unique · Visible · Spaced 20–30 NM
