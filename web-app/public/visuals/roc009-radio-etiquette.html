ROC-009 — Radio Etiquette

ISED RIC-21 · AIM COM 4.0 · ICAO Annex 10 Vol. II · ROC-A Written Exam

Core Rules
Rule 1

Listen before transmitting

Monitor the frequency for at least 5 seconds before keying the PTT. Transmitting while others are talking causes both transmissions to be lost (stepped-on). This is the single most important habit.

Rule 2

Keep transmissions brief

Radio time is shared. Plan what you're going to say before pressing PTT. Avoid filler words, pauses mid-transmission, and unnecessary repetition. State who, where, what — then release.

Rule 3

Use standard phraseology

ICAO/ISED standard words carry precise meaning. Plain language is permitted when standard phrases don't cover the situation, but standard phrases reduce ambiguity and speed up communication.

Rule 4

Speak at a moderate pace

Speak clearly at a conversational pace — not fast (hard to write down) and not slow (wastes airtime). Slow down slightly when giving alphanumeric strings (frequencies, squawk codes, clearances).

Rule 5

Correct microphone technique

Hold the mic 2–5 cm from your mouth. Press PTT, pause 1 second before speaking (to let the transmitter activate), then speak. Release PTT only after finishing. Avoid wind and breathing noise.

Rule 6

Read back mandatory items

Always read back: runway, take-off/landing clearance, hold-short instructions, heading, altitude, altimeter, transponder code, and frequency changes. Omitting a readback signals non-receipt to ATC.

Rule 7

Callsign every transmission

End every transmission with your callsign (abbreviated after first contact). This lets ATC and other traffic identify who just transmitted, especially important on busy tower frequencies.

Rule 8

"Standby" buys time

If you cannot respond immediately (head-down, busy), say "Standby, [callsign]" and ATC will wait. Never leave ATC without an acknowledgement — a missed call may lead ATC to assume a radio failure.

Do vs Don't

✓ Do

  • Use phonetic alphabet for all letters"Golf Charlie Delta Foxtrot" not "G-C-D-F"
  • Say "say again" to request a repeat"Say again altitude, C-GCDF"
  • State "Correction" then re-transmit if you made an error
  • Use "Affirmative" and "Negative" for yes/no
  • Say "Wilco" if you intend to complyMeans "understood, will comply"
  • Cancel VFR flight plan when safely on ground
  • Monitor 121.5 MHz whenever a second radio is available

✗ Don't

  • Say "repeat" — this is artillery jargon; say "say again"
  • Transmit personal information, profanity, or music
  • Use "Over and out" — "Over" expects a reply; "Out" ends the call. Don't mix them.
  • Say "Roger" when you mean "Wilco" — "Roger" means received, not agreed
  • Transmit on guard (121.5 MHz) for routine calls
  • Hold PTT without speaking — this blocks the frequency for all users
  • Abbreviate callsign before ATC does so first

SymptomLikely causeAction

EXAM HOOK
Radio etiquette summary: Listen → Think → Transmit → Identify. Never say "repeat" (say say again). "Roger" = received; "Wilco" = will comply. End every call with your callsign. Monitor 121.5 MHz on second radio whenever possible.
