ROC-003 — Aviation Frequency Reference

AIM COM 4.0 · Canada Flight Supplement · ISED RIC-21 · ROC-A Written Exam

Critical Fixed Frequencies
FrequencyTypePurposeNotes
121.5 MHz DISTRESS / GUARD International aeronautical distress; ELT All aircraft should monitor when a second radio is available. ELTs transmit here on impact. (AIM COM 4.3.1)
243.0 MHz MILITARY GUARD Military UHF equivalent of 121.5 MHz Not on civil VHF radios; military aircraft monitor. Noted in AIM.
122.8 MHz UNICOM / MF Non-towered aerodrome traffic advisories; Mandatory Frequency (MF) Self-announce position and intentions at MF aerodromes. (AIM RAC 4.5)
126.7 MHz EN ROUTE / FSS Enroute advisory; FSS relay; air-to-ground Common enroute frequency away from controlled aerodromes. (AIM COM 4.1.4)
123.45 MHz AIR-TO-AIR GA air-to-air coordination Informal; not for ATC contact. Widely used when aircraft are in proximity.
Frequency Bands by Service
Band / RangeServiceTypical use
118.000 – 136.975 MHz
VHF COM		 ATC / COM Tower, approach, departure, ground, centre (ACC). All controlled-airspace communications. 25 kHz channel spacing.
108.000 – 117.975 MHz
VHF NAV		 VOR / ILS / LOC VOR navigation, ILS localizer. NAV radio only — not COM. Odd tenth-decimal = VOR; even = ILS localizer.
ATIS (discrete) ATIS Continuous weather and NOTAM broadcast. Frequency listed per airport in CFS. Obtain ATIS before calling tower.
Ground (discrete) GROUND Taxi instructions at towered airports. Frequency in CFS. Contact before taxiing.
Canada Flight Supplement (CFS): All aerodrome-specific frequencies (ATIS, ground, tower, approach) are listed in the CFS entry for each airport. Always check the CFS before flight — frequencies listed here are system-wide defaults.
