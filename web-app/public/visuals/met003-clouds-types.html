MET-003 — Cloud Types & Formation

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · AIM MET 2.0

Cloud Altitude Bands (Mid-Latitudes)
High Clouds (Cirro-)
Cirrus
CI
Wispy ice-crystal streaks — fair weather, but may signal approaching warm front 24–48 h out.
Cirrostratus
CS
Thin translucent sheet — halo around sun/moon. Warm front signal. Entire sky covered.
Cirrocumulus
CC
"Mackerel sky" — small white ripples high up. Rarely produces precipitation.
Middle Clouds (Alto-)
Altostratus
AS
Grey/blue sheet — sun appears as through frosted glass. Steady rain/snow approaching.
Altocumulus
AC
White/grey patches. AC castellanus → afternoon thunderstorm risk.
Nimbostratus
NS
Dark, ragged, continuous precip. Based mid but often extends very low.
Low Clouds (Strato- / Cumulo-)
Stratus
ST
Uniform low layer — drizzle/mist, poor VFR.
Stratocumulus
SC
Lumpy grey layer with breaks — generally VFR ceilings.
Cumulus
CU
Puffy fair-weather clouds — bumpy below, smooth above base.
Cumulonimbus
CB
Towering thunderstorm cell — avoid by 20 NM. All known hazards.
Stable vs Unstable Air

Stable Air → Stratiform

  • Smooth layered clouds
  • Steady precipitation
  • Poor visibility in precip
  • Smooth flight, but low ceilings
  • Icing risk in cloud < 0 °C

Unstable Air → Cumuliform

  • Puffy, vertical clouds
  • Showery precipitation
  • Good vis between showers
  • Turbulent, gusty winds
  • Thunderstorms & hail possible

FormulaWorked Example
Base AGL ≈ (T − Td) × 400 ft Surface T 22 °C, Td 14 °C → spread 8 °C → base ≈ 3,200 ft AGL
EXAM HOOK
Strat- = layered & stable (smooth ride, low vis). Cumulo- = heaped & unstable (bumpy, showers). Nimbo-/-nimbus = precipitation. Alto- = middle level. Cirro- = high & icy. A CB contains every hazard known to aviation — avoid by 20 NM.
