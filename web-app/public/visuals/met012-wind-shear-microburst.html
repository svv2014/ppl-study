MET-012 — Wind Shear & Microburst

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · AIM MET 2.7 · LLWS = within 2,000 ft AGL

Microburst Side-View — Approach Profile
Classic microburst "trap": the initial performance boost is followed by a dramatic loss — power may be insufficient to recover close to the ground.
Three Stages a Pilot Experiences
1. Headwind gain — airspeed and lift increase; pilot reduces power, trims nose-down to hold glidepath.
2. Downdraft / calm — sudden sink rate; aircraft drops below path.
3. Tailwind loss — airspeed and lift collapse; stall or ground contact imminent.
Wet vs Dry Microburst
TypeClueSource
WetHeavy rain shaft to the surfaceMature thunderstorm with high liquid water.
DryRain evaporates before ground; visible virga only. Dust ring on surface.High-based convection in dry air — prairie summers.

EXAM HOOK
LLWS = low-level wind shear, below 2,000 ft AGL — most dangerous where no altitude exists to trade for airspeed. A microburst cycle on approach goes headwind → downdraft → tailwind. The initial headwind boost can fool you into cutting power — don't. Any virga below a convective cloud in dry air means a possible dry microburst.
