ROC-A — Emergency & Urgency Procedures

Canadian AIM RAC · Transport Canada · ROC-A Quick Reference

Distress Call — MAYDAY
MAYDAY
Distress — Grave and Imminent Danger
Spoken 3 times. Use 121.5 MHz if unable to reach ATC. (AIM RAC 1.7)
  • 1Distress signal (×3): "MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY"
  • 2Station addressed: Name of station (or "ALL STATIONS" if unknown)
  • 3Aircraft identification: Call sign
  • 4Nature of distress: Engine failure / fire / structural / etc.
  • 5Intentions: Forced landing / ditching / etc.
  • 6Position: Last known fix, bearing and distance from navaid, or lat/long
  • 7Altitude: Current altitude / flight level
  • 8Persons on board (POB): Number of souls on board
  • 9Any other information: Fuel remaining, ELT status, aircraft colour, etc.
Example: "MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY, Ottawa Centre, Cessna Golf-Charlie-Foxtrot-Bravo, engine failure, forced landing, 15 miles south of Ottawa, 2,500 feet, 2 POB, Mayday."
Urgency Call — PAN-PAN
PAN-PAN
Urgency — Safety at Risk (Not Yet Grave)
Spoken 3 times. Used for medical, lost, low fuel, etc. (AIM RAC 1.7)
  • 1Urgency signal (×3): "PAN-PAN PAN-PAN PAN-PAN"
  • 2Station addressed: Name of station or "ALL STATIONS"
  • 3Aircraft identification: Call sign
  • 4Nature of urgency: Medical / lost / fuel state / etc.
  • 5Intentions: What you plan to do
  • 6Position: Best known position
  • 7Altitude: Current altitude
  • 8Persons on board: Souls on board
  • 9Any other information: Relevant details
Emergency Transponder Codes
7500 HIJACK Unlawful interference. Do NOT squawk unless actually under threat — ATC will immediately alert security services.
7600 LOST COMMS Radio failure. Squawk 7600 and follow lost-comms procedures below.
7700 EMERGENCY General emergency. Squawk 7700 with any MAYDAY situation; retains last assigned squawk on secondary systems.
Lost Communications — Step-by-Step
1 Squawk 7600 on the transponder to alert ATC of radio failure.
2 Try all frequencies — attempt to re-establish contact on last assigned, guard (121.5 MHz), and other known frequencies.
3 Check equipment — confirm volume, headset plugs, avionics master switch, breakers, and that squelch is not too high.
4 Listen and look — ATC may continue to give instructions; watch for light signals from the control tower (see Light Signals reference card).
5 If in controlled airspace: remain in VMC if possible; follow the expected routing and altitude. Proceed to destination, rock wings to acknowledge light signals.
6 Arrival at controlled aerodrome: Enter the circuit normally; watch the tower for a steady green light signal (cleared to land). Rock wings to confirm receipt.
7 After landing: Telephone ATC immediately to report the failure. (AIM RAC 1.9, CARs 602.136)
MAYDAY vs PAN-PAN: MAYDAY = grave and imminent danger requiring immediate assistance. PAN-PAN = urgent but not yet catastrophic — time to think and plan. Upgrade a PAN-PAN to MAYDAY if the situation deteriorates. Cancel either with "MAYDAY/PAN-PAN [call sign] cancel distress/urgency." (Source: AIM RAC 1.7)
