MET-011 — Turbulence

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Chapter 8 · PPL Written Exam

Turbulence Intensity — Cockpit Effects and PIREP Reporting
Intensity PIREP Code Cockpit Effects Aircraft Attitude Action Required
Light LGT Slight erratic changes in altitude or attitude; occupants feel slight strain against seatbelts; unsecured items show slight movement Slight deviation from wings level; immediate correction possible Monitor Report; slow to turbulence penetration speed if sustained
Moderate MOD Definite strain against seatbelts; difficult to walk; unsecured items displaced; food/beverage service impossible Definite but controllable changes in attitude and altitude Seatbelts Brief ATC; reduce to turbulence penetration speed (Va); consider altitude change
Severe SEV Large abrupt changes; aircraft momentarily out of control; occupants forced violently against seatbelts; loose items thrown about Large deviations in altitude and attitude; possible brief loss of control Emergency action Slow to Va immediately; issue UUA PIREP; divert if unable to exit
Extreme EXTM Violent; aircraft practically impossible to control; may cause structural damage Violent pitch and roll; uncontrollable momentarily Structural damage possible UUA PIREP; reduce power; maintain wings level; do not fight
Turbulence Types
CAT — Clear Air Turbulence
Altitude
Above 15,000 ft; typically near jet stream (30,000–40,000 ft)
Cause
Wind shear between air masses moving at different speeds; jet stream edges
Warning signs
NONE — no visual cue, no cloud, no precipitation indicator
Severity
MOD to SEV possible
Avoidance
PIREP data; GFA icing/turbulence chart; fly perpendicular to jet stream to exit
Mechanical Turbulence
Altitude
Low level — surface to 3,000 ft AGL
Cause
Wind flowing over irregular terrain, buildings, trees; friction layer disruption
Warning signs
Gusty surface winds; terrain features; smoke patterns
Severity
LGT to MOD typical
Avoidance
Increase approach speed; extend pattern altitude; avoid low-level flight in strong winds
Orographic / Mountain Wave
Altitude
Can extend from surface to 100,000+ ft; worst near and downwind of ridgeline
Cause
Wind perpendicular to ridge at > 25 kt causes oscillating waves downwind
Warning signs
Lenticular (lens-shaped) clouds; rotor clouds below wave crest; cap cloud on ridge
Severity
SEV to EXTM in rotor zone
Avoidance
Stay above wave crest altitude; cross ridgelines at 45 degrees; avoid rotor zone; check PIREPs
Convective Turbulence
Altitude
Low to mid-level; inside and near cumulus/Cb
Cause
Thermal convection; strong updrafts and downdrafts inside convective cloud
Warning signs
Cumulus buildups; afternoon heating; cumulonimbus
Severity
MOD to EXTM in Cb
Avoidance
Avoid Cb by 20 NM; fly early morning before thermal development; monitor radar
Wake Turbulence
Altitude
Below the generating aircraft; sink at ~300–500 fpm; spread laterally
Cause
Counter-rotating vortices shed from wingtips of heavy aircraft; strongest when heavy, slow, clean config
Warning signs
Heavy aircraft departure/arrival; no visual cue for the vortices themselves
Severity
SEV to EXTM for small aircraft
Avoidance
Maintain separation; stay above heavy aircraft flight path; land beyond touchdown point; takeoff before rotation point
Frontal Turbulence
Altitude
Near frontal boundaries; most intense near cold front
Cause
Wind shear at frontal surface; instability in prefrontal air; cold front squall lines
Warning signs
Cold front passage; squall line; rapid pressure change
Severity
MOD; SEV in squall line
Avoidance
Cross fronts at right angles and quickly; check SIGMET for squall line positions
Mountain Wave — Key Details
Feature Description
Formation requirement Wind > 25 kt near perpendicular to ridge; stable air aloft; wind speed increases with altitude
Horizontal extent Waves can extend 100–300 NM downwind of the ridge; do not assume safety just because you are far from mountains
Lenticular cloud (ACSL) Altocumulus Standing Lenticular — stationary lens-shaped cloud at wave crest; indicates active wave. Does not move with wind.
Rotor zone Violent turbulent eddies under the wave crest; can be below ridgeline altitude; most dangerous area; associated with rotor cloud
Cap cloud Cloud cap on ridge crest; indicates moist air being lifted over the ridge; tops may indicate wave height
PIREP reporting Report location, altitude, intensity (LGT/MOD/SEV), and aircraft type. Mountain wave PIREPs are critical for following aircraft.

Memory Hook — Turbulence Essentials

CATNo visual warning — clear air, no cloud, no radar return. Only indicator: PIREPs and GFA turbulence chart. Happens near jet stream.
MOUNTAIN WAVECan extend hundreds of miles downwind of ridgeline. Lenticular cloud = wave active. Rotor zone = most violent. Cross ridges at 45 degrees.
Va SPEEDIn turbulence, reduce to maneuvering speed (Va). Below Va, aircraft will stall before structural failure. Va decreases with lighter weight.
WAKE VORTEXVortices sink and spread laterally. In crosswind, upwind vortex stays on runway. Stay above and upwind of heavy aircraft. Most dangerous: calm wind.
