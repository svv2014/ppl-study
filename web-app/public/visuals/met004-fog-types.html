MET-004 — Fog Formation & Types

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · AIM MET 2.0 · Fog = visibility < ½ SM (vs mist/BR)

The Five Fog Types

Radiation Fog

RADIATION
TriggerOvernight cooling of ground; clear sky, calm/light wind, moist air.
WhereLand, low-lying valleys. Not over water.
ClearsMorning sun heats surface (burns off by mid-morning).

Advection Fog

ADVECTION
TriggerWarm, moist air flows over cooler surface.
WhereMaritime coasts (e.g., East Coast summer). Can form day or night.
ClearsWind shift or air-mass change — may persist for days.

Upslope Fog

UPSLOPE
TriggerMoist air forced up sloping terrain; adiabatic cooling saturates it.
WherePrairies east of Rockies, Appalachian slopes.
ClearsWind shift away from slope.

Steam Fog

STEAM / SEA-SMOKE
TriggerCold, dry air flows over much warmer water.
WhereGreat Lakes in autumn, Arctic leads, any warm water in cold air.
ClearsStops when water cools or air warms.

Precipitation / Frontal Fog

PRECIPITATION
TriggerWarm rain falling through cool air saturates it; evaporation adds moisture.
WhereAhead of warm fronts or stalled fronts.
ClearsFront passage or precipitation ending.

Ice Fog

ICE (BONUS)
TriggerExtreme cold (< −30 °C) — water vapour sublimates directly to ice crystals.
WhereArctic & subarctic Canada in winter, near towns (combustion moisture).
ClearsSlowly; often requires temperature rise.

EXAM HOOK
Radiation fog needs three things: clear sky, calm wind, high humidity. Remove any one and it doesn't form. Advection fog forms when warm moist air moves over a cold surface — it's the only type that tolerates steady wind up to ~15 kt. Any fog reduces vis below ½ SM — above that, it's mist (BR) or haze (HZ).
