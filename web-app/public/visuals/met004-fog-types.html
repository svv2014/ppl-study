MET-004 — Fog Types

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Chapter 8 · PPL Written Exam

Fog Type Reference Table
Fog Type Formation Mechanism Typical Conditions Aviation Hazard Dissipation
Radiation Fog Ground radiates heat to sky after sunset, cooling the air above to dew point Clear night, light wind (1–5 kt), moist air, calm conditions. Most common in valleys. Severe Can reduce visibility to near zero overnight; forms rapidly Burns off after sunrise (1–3 hr) as ground warms. Wind also disperses it.
Advection Fog Warm moist air moves (advects) over a cooler surface and is cooled to dew point Warm air over cold ocean, snow-covered ground, or cold lake. Common on Canadian coasts. Persistent Can cover large areas; not dependent on time of day; does NOT burn off at sunrise Requires change in wind direction to remove the warm moist air source
Upslope Fog Moist air forced up terrain cools adiabatically until it reaches dew point Moist air flowing toward elevated terrain; common in foothills (e.g. Alberta Rockies approach) Terrain Combined low visibility and elevated terrain; disorients VFR pilots Clears when flow changes or air mass dries; can persist for extended periods
Steam Fog Cold dry air moves over warm water; water evaporates and immediately re-condenses Arctic air over open water (lakes in autumn, coastal areas). Looks like steam rising. Moderate Low-level wisps; can reduce visibility over water; turbulence possible Dissipates as air mass warms or moves inland; generally shallow
Freezing Fog Radiation or advection fog that exists at temperatures below 0°C; supercooled droplets Any fog conditions when OAT at or below 0°C Extreme Deposits clear ice on aircraft surfaces, windscreens, and runways; aircraft icing hazard Dissipates with temperature rise above 0°C or change in air mass
Radiation vs. Advection — Key Differences
Radiation Fog
Trigger
Nighttime ground cooling (radiative)
Wind requirement
Calm to light (1–5 kt). Strong wind mixes it away.
Location
Inland, valleys, low-lying areas
Time of occurrence
Night to early morning; clears after sunrise
Prediction
Predictable — check dew point spread at sunset
Area covered
Local / regional (valley scale)
Advection Fog
Trigger
Warm moist air moving over cold surface
Wind requirement
Needs wind to advect the moist air — wind does NOT clear it
Location
Coastal, offshore, areas with cold ocean currents
Time of occurrence
Any time of day or night; does NOT clear at sunrise
Prediction
Harder to predict; requires knowledge of air mass trajectory
Area covered
Can cover very large areas (hundreds of km)
Forecasting Fog — Dew Point Spread Rule
Dew Point Spread (OAT minus Dew Point) Interpretation Action
> 5°C Clear skies likely Air far from saturation No immediate fog concern; monitor trend
3 – 5°C Monitor closely Approaching saturation if cooling continues Check hourly METAR trend, especially at sunset
≤ 2°C Fog expected Near or at saturation point Expect fog; plan alternate; consider delay or divert
0°C (spread = 0) Fog or cloud present now Do not depart VFR; obtain current METAR + ATIS

Memory Hook — Fog Types at a Glance

RADIATIONClear + calm + cool night + moist air = radiation fog by morning. Clears after sunrise. Valley airports: brief operations delay.
ADVECTIONWarm air over cold surface = advection fog. Coastal hazard. Sunrise does NOT clear it. Needs wind shift to dissipate.
FREEZINGAny fog below 0°C = freezing fog. Supercooled droplets freeze on contact. Aircraft icing hazard even on the ground.
DEW POINTSpread ≤ 2°C = fog likely. Spread = 0 = saturated. Check METAR reported as TEMP/DP (e.g. 12/10 = 2°C spread).
