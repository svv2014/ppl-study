Altitude and Frequency Conventions
What you readWhat you sayRule
3,500 ft"Tree thousand fife hundred"Each number group spoken separately
FL240"Flight level two four zero"Three digits spoken individually
122.8 MHz"One two two decimal eight"Decimal point = "decimal"
Squawk 7700"Squawk seven seven zero zero"Each digit individually
Runway 28L"Runway two eight left"Two-digit designator
Wind 270°/15 kt"Wind two seven zero at one fife"Three digits for direction
Why "Niner" and "Tree"? "Niner" prevents confusion with the German word Nein (no). "Tree" avoids misreading "three" as "free" on a noisy radio. "Fife" reduces confusion with "fire." These deviations are ICAO-mandated, not optional. (ICAO Annex 10 Vol. II §5.2.1.7)

Memory Hook

3Tree — not "three"
5Fife — not "five"
9Niner — not "nine"
.Decimal point → say "decimal" (never "point")
FLFL240 → "two four zero" — never "two hundred forty"
