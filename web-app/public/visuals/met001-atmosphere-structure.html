MET-001 — The Atmosphere

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Chapter 8 · PPL Written Exam

Atmospheric Layers — Space to Surface
Exosphere
Above 500 km (1,640,000 ft)
Outer space transition
Molecules escape to space. No defined upper limit. Not relevant to aviation operations.
Thermosphere
80 – 500 km (260,000 – 1,640,000 ft)
Ionosphere / aurora
Temperatures rise dramatically with altitude. Contains the ionosphere; supports radio wave propagation.
Mesosphere
50 – 80 km (164,000 – 260,000 ft)
Coldest layer
Temperatures decrease with altitude, reaching -90°C at the mesopause. Meteors burn up here.
Stratosphere
11 – 50 km (36,000 – 164,000 ft)
Stable / ozone layer
Very stable. Contains ozone layer (~25 km). Temperature constant then rises with altitude. Jet aircraft cruise here. Tropopause at base caps thunderstorms.
Troposphere
0 – 11 km (Surface to ~36,000 ft)
Varies: ~26,000 ft poles / ~60,000 ft equator
All weather here
Contains 75% of atmospheric mass. Temperature decreases with altitude at ~2°C per 1,000 ft (ISA). All significant weather, clouds, and flight operations occur in this layer.
ISA — International Standard Atmosphere (Sea Level)
Parameter ISA Value Units (alternate) Aviation Significance
Sea-Level Temperature 15°C 59°F Baseline for density altitude calculations
Sea-Level Pressure 1013.25 hPa 29.92 inHg Standard altimeter setting; used for Flight Levels
Temperature Lapse Rate 2°C per 1,000 ft 6.5°C / 1,000 m Expected temperature at any given altitude in ISA
Sea-Level Density 1.225 kg/m³ — Reference for aerodynamic performance calculations
Tropopause (mid-latitude) ~36,000 ft / 11 km — Upper limit of weather; caps convective cloud tops

Memory Hook — Atmosphere Essentials

TROPOSPHEREWeather layer — all clouds, turbulence, icing, and VFR/IFR flight happen here. Temperature drops ~2°C per 1,000 ft.
TROPOPAUSECap on thunderstorm tops — Cb anvils spread sideways when they hit the stable stratosphere. Signals extreme wx below.
ISA15°C / 1013.25 hPa / 2°C per 1,000 ft — the three numbers you must memorize for altimetry, performance, and exam questions.
LAYERSFrom surface up: Tropo → Strato → Meso → Thermo → Exo. Mnemonic: Tropical Storms Make The Earth vibrate.
