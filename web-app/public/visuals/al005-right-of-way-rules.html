AL-005 — Right-of-Way Rules

Transport Canada · CARs 602.19–602.21, TP 12880E · PPL Written Exam

Aircraft Category Hierarchy — Higher = More Right-of-Way
1 Balloons Right of Way over All Cannot manoeuvre on demand
2 Gliders Over Airships, Tow, Powered No engine — limited manoeuvring
3 Airships Over towing aircraft and powered aircraft
4 Aircraft towing other aircraft or objects Over powered aircraft only
5 Powered Aircraft Lowest Priority — Yields to All Above
Scenario Quick Reference — CARs 602.19–602.21
Scenario Who Gives Way Rule / Source
Powered aircraft vs. glider (converging) Powered aircraft Category hierarchy — CARs 602.19(1)
Two powered aircraft converging at same altitude Aircraft that has the other on its right Give way to traffic on your right — CARs 602.19(2)
Head-on approach (collision risk) Both aircraft turn right Both turn simultaneously — CARs 602.20
Overtaking (from behind, within 70° of tail) Overtaking aircraft — passes to the right, yields Being-overtaken aircraft holds course — CARs 602.21
Aircraft on final approach / landing All other aircraft yield to the landing aircraft Lower on approach = higher priority — CARs 602.19(3)
Two aircraft on approach at different altitudes Higher aircraft yields to the lower aircraft CARs 602.19(3)
Aircraft declaring emergency (MAYDAY / PAN-PAN) All other aircraft yield — absolute priority Emergency overrides all other rules

Exam Decision Flow — Work Through in Order

Step 1Different aircraft categories? Apply category hierarchy — powered aircraft yields to glider, balloon, etc.
Step 2Is one aircraft on approach to land? Landing aircraft has priority over all others.
Step 3Same category, converging? The aircraft that has the other on its RIGHT gives way.
Step 4Head-on? Both aircraft turn right — simultaneously.
Step 5Overtaking? Overtaking aircraft passes to the right and yields. Being-overtaken holds course.
