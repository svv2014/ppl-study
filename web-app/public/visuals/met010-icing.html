MET-010 — Aircraft Icing

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Chapter 8 · PPL Written Exam

Conditions Required for Structural Icing
Requirement Specific Condition Notes
Visible Moisture Cloud, fog, freezing rain, drizzle, wet snow Clear air cannot cause structural icing. Carb icing can occur in clear air (different mechanism).
Temperature at or below 0°C OAT 0°C down to approximately -40°C Most severe icing: -2°C to -10°C (large supercooled droplets). Below -40°C droplets freeze before impact.
Aircraft surface below 0°C Airframe temperature must be at or below freezing Flying into cloud above freezing: no structural icing even if droplets exist
Icing Types — Formation and Hazard
Clear Ice (Glaze)
Temperature range
0°C to -10°C
Droplet size
Large supercooled droplets
Appearance
Transparent, glossy, smooth surface
Adhesion
Extremely hard; bonds firmly to surface
Hazard level
Most hazardous
Removal
Hardest to remove; can bridge gaps in de-ice boots
Where found
Freezing rain, base of convective clouds
Rime Ice
Temperature range
-10°C to -20°C
Droplet size
Small supercooled droplets
Appearance
White, milky, rough; looks like frost
Adhesion
Brittle; relatively easier to remove
Hazard level
Significant
Removal
Brittle — pneumatic boots more effective
Where found
Stratiform clouds, light freezing drizzle
Mixed Ice
Temperature range
0°C to -15°C (transitional zone)
Droplet size
Combination of large and small droplets
Appearance
Combination of clear and white portions; irregular shape
Adhesion
Irregular shape disrupts airflow severely
Hazard level
Most aerodynamically disruptive
Removal
Difficult; irregular shape unpredictable
Where found
Convective clouds, areas of mixed droplet sizes

Intensity PIREP Code Accumulation Rate Operational Impact Action Required
None NEG No accumulation No effect Continue normally
Trace TRC Barely perceptible Minimal Monitor; de-icing not required unless exposed > 1 hr
Light LGT Slow accumulation Manageable De-icing adequate; consider altitude change
Moderate MOD Rapid accumulation Performance degraded Immediate change of altitude or route; de-icing may not be adequate

Structural Icing Locations on Aircraft
Wings and Tail
Leading edge ice changes airfoil shape; increases stall speed; reduces lift; may cause asymmetric lift
Pitot Tube
Ice blocks pitot orifice; airspeed indication fails (reads zero or stuck); critical for flight instruments
Propeller
Ice on prop blades; unbalanced shedding causes severe vibration; efficiency loss; thrust reduction
Carburettor
Carburettor icing: can occur at temperatures from -20°C to +38°C in humid conditions — even clear air. Engine roughness first sign.
Antennas
Comms and nav antenna iced over; potential radio/GPS failure; adds drag
Windscreen
Forward visibility lost; crew unable to see for visual approaches or obstacle avoidance
Control Surfaces
Ice in hinge gaps restricts control movement; control forces increase; may jam in extreme cases
Static Ports
Ice blocks static source; altimeter, VSI, airspeed errors result. Alternate static source required.

Memory Hook — Aircraft Icing

TWO REQUIREMENTSVisible moisture + temperature at or below 0°C. Both must be present. No moisture = no structural icing (carburettor icing is different).
WORST RANGEMost severe icing at -2°C to -10°C. Large supercooled droplets stay liquid and freeze on impact as clear ice.
ICE TYPESClear = hardest to remove (hard/transparent). Rime = brittle/white. Mixed = worst aerodynamic disruption (irregular shape).
CARB ICINGCarburettor icing does NOT need visible moisture or freezing OAT. Can occur +38°C to -20°C with humidity. Apply carb heat at first engine roughness.
