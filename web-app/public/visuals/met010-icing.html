MET-010 — Structural Icing

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · AIM MET 2.4 · Two conditions required: visible moisture + OAT ≤ 0 °C

Icing Temperature Envelope
Three Ice Types

Rime

−10 °C to −40 °C · small droplets
  • Milky, opaque, rough
  • Freezes on impact; traps air
  • Mostly in stratus clouds
  • Breaks off cleanly when shed

Clear / Glaze

0 °C to −10 °C · large droplets
  • Smooth, transparent, heavy
  • Spreads over wing before freezing
  • Cumuliform cloud & freezing rain
  • Most dangerous — hard to shed

Mixed

−10 °C to −15 °C · varied droplets
  • Irregular, brittle, rough surface
  • Worst aerodynamic shape
  • Common in TCU with varied drop sizes
Effects on the Aircraft
Weight ↑ — reduces useful load, climb performance.
Lift ↓ — wing contour disrupted, stall speed rises.
Drag ↑ — substantial; cruise speed falls, fuel burn climbs.
Thrust ↓ — blocked carb/inlet, iced prop imbalance.
Control ↓ — frozen hinges, jammed surfaces.
Instruments ↓ — pitot/static block, false readings.
Pilot Actions
ConditionAction
Freezing rain / drizzleClimb to above-freezing air, or descend below cloud immediately; turn back.
In-cloud icing startsChange altitude to exit cloud or find warmer air (check freezing-level chart).
Suspected pitot/static icePitot heat ON, alt. static source if equipped; cross-check GPS ground speed.
Suspected induction / carb iceCarb heat full ON; watch for initial RPM drop then recovery.
Any severe icingDeclare & divert; file a UUA PIREP.
EXAM HOOK
Two conditions are required for structural icing: visible moisture AND OAT ≤ 0 °C. Highest risk band is 0 to −15 °C. Freezing rain means warmer air above — climb to escape (or turn back). Most non-ice-protected GA aircraft are prohibited from flight into known icing conditions.
