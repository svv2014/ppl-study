GK-011 — Takeoff & Landing Performance

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · PPL Written Exam

Reference Speeds
Vr
ROTATION SPEED
Speed at which pilot applies back pressure to lift nose off runway. Aircraft becomes airborne shortly after.
Vx
BEST ANGLE OF CLIMB
Maximum altitude gain per unit of horizontal distance. Used for obstacle clearance after takeoff. Slower than Vy.
Vy
BEST RATE OF CLIMB
Maximum altitude gain per unit of time (highest ft/min). Normal climb speed after clearing obstacles.
Factors Affecting Performance
FactorTakeoff EffectLanding Effect
Higher weightLonger ground roll; higher Vr; reduced climb rateHigher approach speed; longer landing roll
Higher density altitudeSignificantly longer ground roll; reduced accelerationHigher true airspeed at landing; longer roll
HeadwindShorter ground roll (subtract ~10% ground roll per 10% headwind)Shorter approach; shorter landing roll
TailwindSignificantly longer ground roll (add ~20% per 10% headwind-equivalent)Longer approach; much longer landing roll
Upslope runwayLonger takeoff roll (gravity opposing)Shorter landing roll (gravity assisting braking)
Downslope runwayShorter takeoff rollLonger landing roll (gravity works against braking)
Soft/grass surfaceLonger roll due to increased rolling resistance (+15–25%)Shorter roll (surface friction increases stopping)
Wet/icy surfaceSimilar to soft; may reduce tire tractionLonger roll — reduced braking friction on wet/ice
Flap settingPartial flap: shorter roll, lower Vr, better angle; full flap: may reduce performanceFull flap: steeper approach, shorter landing roll
Rule of Thumb — Tailwind: A tailwind of 10% of liftoff speed approximately increases takeoff distance by 20% and landing distance by 10%. Exam questions often test tailwind penalty. Always use actual POH charts — never rely on rules of thumb for actual flight planning. V1 (decision speed) applies to multi-engine aircraft only; not relevant for single-engine PPL.
