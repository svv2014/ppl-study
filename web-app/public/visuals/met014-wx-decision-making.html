Go / No-Go Decision Framework
  • 1
    Obtain a complete weather brief
    Call 1-866-WXBRIEF (NAV CANADA) or use NavCAN.ca. Get briefing for departure, en route, and destination. Request PIREPs for your route.
  • 2
    Check METAR at departure and destination
    Current ceiling, visibility, wind, temperature/dew point spread. Is the destination currently VMC? What is the trend?
  • 3
    Check TAF for destination and alternates
    What are forecast conditions at your ETA? Are TEMPO or PROB30/40 items significant for your flight?
  • 4
    Review GFA for the route
    Check Clouds & Weather chart (ceiling/visibility) and Icing/Turbulence chart. Identify freezing level. Mark hazard areas on your chart.
  • 5
    Identify all hazards explicitly
    List: icing risk, turbulence areas, IFR conditions, frontal positions, fog potential. Do not rationalize hazards away.
  • 6
    Identify alternate aerodromes
    Select one or more alternates with better forecast conditions. Confirm fuel is adequate to reach alternate. Know when to divert.
  • 7
    Apply personal minimums — make the GO/NO-GO decision
    Compare conditions to your personal minimums (not just legal minimums). When in doubt, do not go. State your decision out loud.
IMSAFE Checklist — Personal Airworthiness
I
Illness
Any symptoms of illness — cold, flu, GI, headache? Even minor illness degrades performance and may affect pressurization/altitude tolerance.
"Am I feeling physically well today?"
M
Medication
Any prescription or OTC medication? Many common drugs are prohibited for flight or cause drowsiness, impaired judgement, or vestibular effects.
"Am I on any medication that could affect my flying?"
S
Stress
Personal, financial, family, or work stress occupies mental bandwidth needed for flying. Stress degrades decision-making and situational awareness.
"Is anything on my mind that will distract me?"
A
Alcohol
CARs 602.03: No alcohol within 8 hours of flight. Blood alcohol must not exceed 0.04%. Hangover effects persist well beyond 8 hours. When in doubt, do not fly.
"Have I had alcohol in the last 8+ hours?"
F
Fatigue
Fatigue impairs judgement as severely as alcohol. Less than 6 hours of sleep, night shift work, or disrupted sleep patterns all increase risk significantly.
"Am I rested enough to fly safely for the full flight duration?"
E
Emotion
Emotional upset (grief, anger, anxiety, excitement) impairs cognitive function. Strong emotions narrow attention and reduce risk perception accuracy.
"Am I emotionally calm enough to make good decisions under pressure?"

Continuation Bias — The #1 Killer in VFR Weather

DEFINITIONContinuation bias is the tendency to continue a plan of action despite deteriorating conditions or new information that should trigger a change. The earlier decision to "go" creates pressure to stay committed.
HOW IT KILLSVFR pilot encounters worsening weather en route. Instead of turning back, presses on hoping it will improve. Visibility drops. Enters IMC. Spatial disorientation. Controlled flight into terrain (CFIT).
SIGNS"It'll clear up soon." / "I've come this far." / "The passengers are counting on me." / "I don't want to look foolish by turning back." / "Just a little further."
ANTIDOTEEstablish a pre-flight decision trigger: "If visibility drops below [X] SM or ceiling below [Y] ft, I turn back immediately." Commit to it before you depart. Not negotiable in flight.
Personal Minimums — The Concept
Standard Meaning Example Application
Legal Minimum The floor set by CARs — the absolute minimum permitted by regulation 3 SM visibility, 500 ft below cloud in controlled airspace Floor only — designed for proficient, experienced pilots in appropriate aircraft
Personal Minimum Your self-imposed limit that provides a safety buffer above legal minimums Student pilot: 5 SM vis, 2,000 ft ceiling, wind < 15 kt Your real limit — accounts for your experience, currency, aircraft, and the specific flight
Currency Recent experience — have you flown this type of operation recently? 3 takeoffs/landings in last 90 days for passenger carrying; recent night currency Legal currency is the minimum — raise minimums if not recently current in conditions

Memory Hook — Weather Decision-Making

IMSAFEIllness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion. Check yourself before every flight. One YES = consider no-go or reduced complexity flight.
LEGAL vs PERSONALLegal minimum = floor. Personal minimum = YOUR limit. Personal minimums must always be higher than legal. They decrease as experience grows.
CONTINUATION BIASSet your turn-back triggers before you depart. Never negotiate with yourself in flight. "When in doubt, don't" applies to en route weather decisions too.
BRIEF ORDERMETAR (now) → TAF (forecast) → GFA (big picture) → PIREPs (real reports) → Decision. Use all four. No single product tells the whole story.
