NAV-012 — Altitude Types

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Ch.9, Ch.10 · PPL Written Exam

Six Altitude Types — Visual Stack
IA — Indicated Altitude
What the altimeter reads (QNH set)
Altimeter subscale set to current QNH. Reading is approximate height ASL. Most used in flight.
TA — True Altitude
Actual height above sea level
Indicated altitude corrected for temperature deviation from ISA. Used for terrain clearance calculations.
AA — Absolute Altitude
Height above ground (AGL)
True altitude minus terrain elevation. What matters for obstacle clearance in the local area.
PA — Pressure Altitude
Altimeter set to 29.92" Hg (1013.25 hPa)
Used for performance calculations (POH tables). Also the basis for Flight Levels above the transition altitude.
DA — Density Altitude
Pressure altitude corrected for temperature
What the engine and wings "feel." High DA = hot/high/humid = degraded performance. (→ NAV-013)
FL — Flight Level
Pressure altitude in hundreds of feet
Used above the transition altitude (18,000 ft in Canada). Set altimeter to 29.92" Hg. FL180 = 18,000 ft PA.

Cold Temperature Warning — Aircraft Lower Than Indicated

In cold temperatures below ISA, air is denser and the altimeter over-reads. Your aircraft is lower than the altimeter indicates. This matters for instrument approaches and terrain clearance. The colder the temperature (and the higher the altitude), the greater the error. Transport Canada publishes cold temperature correction tables — critical for IFR approaches but also relevant as a PPL concept.

Rule: High to low (temperature) = look out below.

Exam Memory Hooks

AGL vs ASLASL = above sea level (altimeter QNH). AGL = above ground level (how high above the terrain you are). Obstacle charts list heights AGL; altimeters read ASL.
PAPressure Altitude = altimeter set to 29.92" Hg. Always use PA for POH performance charts. PA is independent of local weather — good for comparing performance data.
FLFlight Level = Pressure Altitude ÷ 100. FL185 = 18,500 ft PA. Above the transition altitude (18,000 ft ASL in Canada), all aircraft set 29.92" and use FL notation.
