MET-008 — PIREPs (Pilot Reports)

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Chapter 8 · PPL Written Exam

What is a PIREP?

A PIREP (Pilot Report) is a real-time weather observation submitted by a pilot in flight. PIREPs provide ground-truth data on turbulence, icing, and cloud conditions that cannot be detected by ground stations or radar. They are especially critical in areas with sparse weather reporting and for conditions like Clear Air Turbulence. Pilots are encouraged — and sometimes required — to submit PIREPs when encountering significant weather.

PIREP Format Fields
Field Code Field Name Format / Example Notes
UA / UUA Report Type UA = routine; UUA = urgent UUA issued for SEV turbulence, SEV icing, or other hazards requiring immediate relay
/OV Location /OV YYZ / /OV YYZ-YTZ NAVAID or fix; or between two fixes. Can include bearing/distance (YYZ090025 = 025 NM east of YYZ)
/TM Time /TM 1755 UTC time of observation in HHMM format
/FL Altitude (Flight Level) /FL085 or /FLDURC In hundreds of feet. FLDURC = during climb. FLDURD = during descent.
/TP Aircraft Type /TP C172 ICAO designator. Important — turbulence severity is relative to aircraft size/weight
/SK Sky / Cloud /SK BKN045-TOP065 Cloud layer base-top in hundreds of ft. OVC = overcast. CLR = clear.
/WX Weather /WX FV03SM RA Flight visibility and present weather. FV = flight visibility in SM.
/TA Temperature /TA M08 Outside air temperature in Celsius. M prefix = minus (negative). M08 = -8°C.
/WV Wind /WV 27035KT Wind direction and speed as observed in flight. Direction in degrees true.
/TB Turbulence /TB MOD 070-090 Intensity + altitude band. See intensity table below.
/IC Icing /IC LGT-MOD RIME 060-080 Intensity + type (RIME, CLR, MIXED) + altitude band
/RM Remarks /RM LLWS ON APCH Free text. Common: LLWS (low-level wind shear), ACFT MSK (aircraft masked), DURC (during climb)
Sample PIREP — Decoded Line by Line
UUA → Urgent PIREP (hazardous conditions)
/OV YYZ180030 → Location: 30 NM south of Toronto Pearson (180° bearing)
/TM 1432 → Time: 1432 UTC
/FL085 → Altitude: 8,500 ft
/TP B738 → Aircraft: Boeing 737-800
/SK OVC060-TOP110 → Overcast cloud layer base 6,000 ft, tops 11,000 ft
/TA M06 → Temperature: -6°C (prime icing range)
/TB SEV 080-090 → Severe turbulence between 8,000 and 9,000 ft
/IC MOD CLEAR 070-090 → Moderate clear (glaze) icing between 7,000 and 9,000 ft
/RM LLWS +20KT ON APCH → Remarks: +20 kt wind shear on approach

Memory Hook — PIREP Key Points

UUAUrgent PIREP = SEV or EXTM turbulence, SEV icing, or other critical hazards. ATC relays immediately to other aircraft.
AIRCRAFT TYPEAlways note /TP. SEV turbulence in a B747 may be MOD in a C172. Consider the reporting aircraft size when interpreting.
TEMP + CLOUDIf /TA shows 0°C to -20°C and /SK shows cloud, expect icing. Prime icing range is -2°C to -10°C.
REPORT DUTYWhen you encounter SEV turbulence or icing, report it. PIREPs protect other pilots. Failure to report is a missed safety opportunity.
