MET-002 — Temperature, Pressure & Humidity

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Chapter 8 · PPL Written Exam

Three Key Atmospheric Variables
Temperature
T
Measure of air molecule kinetic energy. Affects air density directly — warm air is less dense. ISA standard: 15°C at sea level.
Pressure
P
Weight of atmosphere above a point. Decreases with altitude. ISA standard: 1013.25 hPa (29.92 inHg) at sea level.
Humidity
RH
Amount of water vapour in air. High humidity reduces air density further. Key indicator: dew point spread.
Concept Reference Table
Concept Definition Key Value / Formula Aviation Relevance
Density Altitude Pressure altitude corrected for non-standard temperature. Represents air density. DA = PA + (120 × (OAT – ISA temp)) Perf. critical High DA = longer takeoff roll, reduced climb, reduced engine power
ISA Temperature Standard temperature at any altitude: 15°C – (2°C × altitude in thousands of ft) 15°C at SL; -57°C at tropopause Compare OAT to ISA to determine if air is warmer (worse DA) or cooler (better DA) than standard
QNH Altimeter setting adjusted to sea level; shows MSL altitude on altimeter Obtained from ATIS/METAR (e.g. A2992 = 29.92 inHg) Standard use Set on ground, altimeter reads field elevation when correct
QFE Altimeter set to field elevation pressure; reads zero on the ground Used in some military/circuit ops Altimeter reads AGL height above that aerodrome
Standard Pressure 29.92 inHg / 1013.25 hPa set on altimeter Required above 18,000 ft ASL (Class A/B) All aircraft use same reference — reads Flight Level (e.g. FL180 = 18,000 ft)
Dew Point Temperature at which air becomes saturated and condensation begins Dew point spread = OAT minus dew point Spread ≤ 2°C: fog or low cloud likely. Spread = 0: saturated / fog present
Relative Humidity Ratio of actual moisture to maximum possible moisture at that temperature 100% = saturated; dew point = OAT High RH lowers air density (water vapour lighter than N2/O2)
Pressure Altitude Altitude indicated when altimeter is set to 29.92 inHg Used as base for density altitude calculation Basis for performance chart lookups when OAT differs from ISA
Temperature Effects on Aircraft Performance
Condition Effect on Air Density Density Altitude Performance Impact
Hot temperature Air expands, fewer molecules per unit volume Higher DA Longer takeoff roll, reduced climb rate, reduced engine output (normally aspirated)
High altitude Less atmosphere above, lower pressure, thinner air Higher DA Same as hot — aircraft "thinks" it is higher than indicated
High humidity Water vapour displaces heavier N2/O2 molecules Higher DA Additional degradation on top of temperature/altitude effects
Cold temperature Air contracts, denser (more molecules per volume) Lower DA Shorter takeoff roll, better climb, more engine power

Memory Hook — Density Altitude Warning

HOT + HIGH + HUMIDTriple H = degraded performance. Each factor independently raises density altitude. All three together can make performance dangerously poor.
DEW POINTSpread ≤ 2°C means fog or cloud is imminent. Spread = 0 means you are in cloud or fog right now.
ALTIMETERQNH (local setting) = MSL altitude on indicator. 29.92 (standard) = Flight Level altitude. Never forget to update QNH when transitioning areas.
