VFR in Controlled Airspace — CARs 602.113

Class C — before entering:

Obtain an ATC clearance (not just radio contact — ATC must say "cleared")
Establish and maintain two-way radio communication on the assigned frequency
Be in VMC — meet Class C VFR weather minimums (CARs 602.114)

Class D — before entering:

Establish two-way radio contact — ATC must acknowledge your call sign (even "standby" counts if your call sign is used)
No formal clearance required — contact alone is sufficient

Class E: no radio contact or clearance required for VFR — simply maintain VMC and comply with altitude rules.

Canadian-Specific Designations: RMZ and TMZ
RMZ — Radio Mandatory Zone

A designated volume of airspace (typically Class G) around an uncontrolled aerodrome where two-way radio communication is mandatory for all aircraft — IFR and VFR alike.

Pilots must monitor the published CTAF or MF frequency and make standard position reports as specified in the Canada Flight Supplement (CFS).

CARs 602.97 · AIP Canada RAC 2.8 · CFS airport entry
TMZ — Transponder Mandatory Zone

A designated volume of airspace where a Mode C (or Mode S) transponder is mandatory for all aircraft, regardless of airspace class, to ensure radar identification in areas of mixed IFR/VFR traffic.

TMZs are published in AIP Canada and charted on VNC/VTA charts. Operating without a transponder in a TMZ requires prior ATC authorization.

CARs 605.35 · AIP Canada RAC 2.8 · VNC/VTA chart notation

⚠️ Class E Trap

Class E is controlled airspace — but VFR pilots need no clearance, no radio, and receive no separation service from ATC. "Controlled" means ATC provides separation services to IFR aircraft there, not that VFR pilots are actively managed. This distinction is a frequent PPL exam trap.
