ROC-008 — Regulatory Framework

Radiocommunication Act · ISED RIC-21 · ICAO Annex 10 · ROC-A Written Exam

Regulatory Pillars

Radiocommunication Act (Canada) ISED

Federal statute administered by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED). Governs all radio transmitters in Canada — including aircraft.

  • Prohibits operating a radio transmitter without a valid licence or authorization
  • Aeronautical radio operators must hold a Restricted Operator Certificate — Aeronautical (ROC-A)
  • Aircraft must hold a Aeronautical Station Licence (issued by ISED) to operate a transmitter on board
  • The ROC-A is an ISED certificate — it is separate from Transport Canada pilot licensing

Restricted Operator Certificate — Aeronautical (ROC-A) ISED

The individual certificate required by any person who operates an aeronautical mobile radio station (the aircraft radio).

  • Examined and issued by ISED-accredited examiners
  • Tests: radio operation, phonetic alphabet, distress procedures, frequencies, regulations
  • Valid for life (no renewal required) once issued
  • Endorsements exist for HF and satellite operations (not required for basic VHF)
  • Not the same as a pilot licence — a non-pilot ground operator needs one too

Aeronautical Station Licence ISED

The station (aircraft) licence for the radio equipment installed in the aircraft.

  • Required for Canadian-registered aircraft operating in Canada and abroad
  • Document must be carried on board while the aircraft is operated
  • The pilot certificate and station licence together satisfy ROC-A operational requirements

Canadian Aviation Regulations (CARs) TC

Transport Canada regulations governing aviation operations, including when radio use is required, airspace communications, and phraseology standards.

  • CARs Part VI sets radio requirements per airspace class
  • Phraseology standards in AIM (Aeronautical Information Manual) supplement the CARs
  • TC enforces CARs; ISED enforces Radiocommunication Act — separate agencies

ICAO Annex 10 — Aeronautical Telecommunications ICAO

International standards adopted by Canada. Sets phonetic alphabet, distress phraseology, and frequency allocations used globally.

  • Annex 10 Vol. II covers communication procedures (phonetic alphabet, readback, MAYDAY)
  • Canada's domestic rules must meet or exceed ICAO standards

ROC-A vs Pilot Licence: The ROC-A (ISED) and the PPL/CPL (Transport Canada) are two separate credentials from two different federal departments. You need both to legally operate an aircraft radio in Canada. A student pilot who has not yet obtained a ROC-A must operate under the supervision of a person who holds one. (Radiocommunication Act s. 13)
EXAM HOOK
Two agencies, two documents: ISED issues the ROC-A (operator certificate) and the Aeronautical Station Licence (aircraft licence). TC issues the pilot licence. The ROC-A exam tests radio operation — it is ISED's exam, not TC's. Log required for commercial ops and all distress communications.
