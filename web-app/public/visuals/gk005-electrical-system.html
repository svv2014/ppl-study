GK-005 — Electrical System

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · PPL Written Exam

System Schematic
Key Components
ComponentDescription
Battery12V (light singles) or 24V. Powers all loads when engine off; enables engine start
AlternatorEngine-driven; primary power source when engine running; charges battery
Voltage RegulatorMaintains constant bus voltage (~14V or ~28V) regardless of engine RPM
Master SwitchTwo-part split switch: Battery half + Alternator half (some aircraft)
Bus BarCentral distribution rail; all loads connect via individual circuit breakers
Circuit BreakersProtect individual circuits; can be reset once (if trip-free type, cannot be held in)
Ammeter vs. Loadmeter
TypeReadsNormal Indication
AmmeterCurrent flow to/from batterySlight positive (charging); zero in steady flight
LoadmeterTotal alternator output loadSome positive reading proportional to electrical load
IndicationMeaningAction
Ammeter dischargingAlternator not charging — battery drainingReduce load; land ASAP
Ammeter high chargeBattery deeply discharged; charging hardMonitor; normal after start
Loadmeter zeroAlternator failureCheck ALT switch; reduce load; land soon
Key Rule: The aircraft electrical system is independent of the ignition magnetos — magnetos operate entirely on their own and will keep the engine running even if all electrical power is lost. Conversely, the engine does NOT need electrical power to keep running once started (except fuel-injected engines with electric fuel pumps). Always reduce electrical load during an alternator failure to extend battery life.
