GK-009 — Weight & Balance

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · PPL Written Exam

CG = Total Moment ÷ Total Weight
Moment = Weight × Arm  |  CG must fall within the approved envelope
Sample W&B Calculation
Item Weight (lbs) Arm (in.) Moment (lb·in)
Empty Aircraft 1,340 87.2 116,848
Pilot (front left) 170 85.0 14,450
Passenger (front right) 150 85.0 12,750
Rear Passengers 0 117.0 0
Baggage 30 140.0 4,200
Fuel (30 USG × 6 lb/USG) 180 96.0 17,280
TOTALS 1,870 — 165,528
CG = 165,528 ÷ 1,870 = 88.5 inches
CG is aft of datum by 88.5 inches — check against envelope limits
Key Definitions
TermDefinition
DatumReference point (often nose of aircraft or firewall) from which all arms are measured. Defined in the POH.
ArmHorizontal distance (inches) from the datum to the item's centre of gravity. Forward = negative (usually); aft = positive.
MomentProduct of weight × arm. Represents the turning force about the datum. Units: lb·in or kg·m.
Centre of Gravity (CG)Point where aircraft balances. Must stay within forward and aft limits for the given gross weight.
Max Gross WeightMaximum allowable total weight — certified structural limit.
Useful LoadMax gross weight minus empty weight. Available for crew, passengers, fuel, baggage.
CG Effects: Forward CG — more stable, nose-heavy, higher stall speed, requires more back pressure, may lack elevator authority at low speeds (e.g. landing flare). Aft CG — less stable, harder to recover from stalls/spins, lighter control feel. Both out-of-limits conditions are illegal and unsafe — the flight must not be conducted.
