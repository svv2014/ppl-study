AL-010 — NOTAMs

Transport Canada · CARs 602.72, AIP Canada GEN 3.1, AIM RAC 2.1 · PPL Written Exam

What is a NOTAM?

A NOTAM (Notice to Air Missions) is a notice containing information essential to personnel concerned with flight operations that is not known sufficiently in advance to be publicised by other means. NOTAMs are mandatory pre-flight briefing material — a pilot-in-command must review all applicable NOTAMs before any flight (CARs 602.72).

NOTAM Types
NOTAM (D)

Distributed NOTAM

Standard NOTAM distributed nationally via the NOTAMplus system. Covers aerodromes, navaids, airspace restrictions, obstacles, and procedures. Most NOTAMs encountered in pre-flight briefings are D NOTAMs.

NOTAM (L)

Local NOTAM

Distributed only to the affected local area. Covers information too minor or local for national distribution — e.g., a taxiway light outage at a small aerodrome. Not always available on national databases.

FDC NOTAM

Flight Data Center NOTAM

Issued by the FAA Flight Data Center (US designation). Covers regulatory or procedural changes — instrument procedure amendments, chart corrections, and TFRs. Canadian pilots encounter FDC NOTAMs when flying to, from, or over US airspace. Regulatory in nature; check carefully before any cross-border operation.

SNOWTAM

Snow / Runway Condition

Special NOTAM series reporting snow, ice, slush, and braking action on runways and taxiways. Critical for winter operations — provides runway condition codes (RCC) per the Global Reporting Format (GRF).

ASHTAM

Volcanic Ash

Special NOTAM series warning of volcanic ash cloud activity. Issued when volcanic ash is a hazard to aviation — provides location, altitude, and forecast movement of ash cloud.

Reading a NOTAM — Worked Example
A1234/25 NOTAMN
Q) CZEG/QMXLC/IV/NBO/A/000/999/5117N11433W005
A) CYYC B) 2503010600 C) 2503312359
E) TWY B BTN TWY C AND TWY D CLSD
Number: A1234/25 — NOTAM number / year
Subject: QMXLC — Q-code subject (MX = taxiway)
Location: CZEG = FIR; CYYC = aerodrome ICAO
Condition: QMXLC — condition code (LC = closed)
Altitude: 000/999 — lower / upper FL in Q-line
Time (effective): B) 2503010600 — 2025 Mar 01 0600Z
Time (expiry): C) 2503312359 — 2025 Mar 31 2359Z
Q-Line Decoded — ICAO Format
Q) CZEG / QMXLC / IV / NBO / A / 000 / 999 / 5117N11433W005
CZEGFIR — Flight Information Region in which the NOTAM applies (Calgary FIR) QMXLCQ-code — subject (MX = taxiway) + condition (LC = closed); always begins with Q IVTraffic — I = IFR, V = VFR (both affected in this case) NBOPurpose — N = nav warning, B = booklet, O = operational; determines how it appears in briefings AScope — A = aerodrome, E = en-route, W = warning, K = checklist 000/999Altitude bounds — lower / upper altitude in hundreds of feet (Flight Level); 000 = surface, 999 = unlimited 5117N11433W005Coordinates + radius — centre point lat/long + radius in nautical miles (005 = 5 NM)

Key Facts for the Exam

MandatoryCARs 602.72 requires the pilot-in-command to review all applicable NOTAMs before flight — this is a legal requirement, not a suggestion.
SNOWTAMA SNOWTAM is a special NOTAM series — not a weather product. It reports runway surface conditions using the Global Reporting Format (GRF) runway condition codes.
Time FormatAll NOTAM times are in UTC (Zulu). The format YYMMDDHHMM is used — e.g., 2503010600 = 2025 March 01 at 0600Z.
PERM vs. ESTIf the expiry field shows PERM, the condition is permanent. EST means estimated — the actual end time may differ.
FDCFDC NOTAMs are a US FAA designation covering regulatory/procedure changes. Always check them when planning cross-border operations into US airspace.
