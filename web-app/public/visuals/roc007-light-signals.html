ROC-007 — Light Signals

CARs 602.101 · AIM RAC 4.5.5 · ICAO Annex 2 · ROC-A Written Exam

Light signals are used when radio communication is unavailable or to communicate with aircraft on the ground or in the circuit. The tower light gun is the authoritative source — obey even if you have radio contact. (CARs 602.101)
Signals from Tower → Aircraft in Flight
Signal Colour / Type Meaning
Steady Green
Continuous green beam Cleared to land

Flashing Red ●●●
Flashing red beam Aerodrome unsafe — do not land
Flashing White ●●●
Flashing white beam Not applicable in flight — used on ground only

Alternating red and green Exercise extreme caution

How to Acknowledge a Light Signal
SituationAircraft in FlightAircraft on Ground (day)Aircraft on Ground (night)
Acknowledge signal received Rock wings (day) or flash landing lights on/off twice (night) Move ailerons or rudder Flash navigation lights on/off twice
Memory pattern — steady vs flashing: Steady signals mean immediate action (cleared or stop). Flashing signals mean conditional or upcoming (return for landing later; taxi; clear the runway). Alternating red/green always means caution regardless of phase. Colour is the dominant cue: Green = go / proceed · Red = stop / danger · White = administrative
EXAM HOOK
Six light signals — both phases:
Steady green: Land (air) / Take off (ground) · Flashing green: Return (air) / Taxi (ground) · Steady red: Circle (air) / Stop (ground) · Flashing red: Unsafe–don't land (air) / Clear runway (ground) · Flashing white: N/A (air) / Return to start (ground) · Alt red/green: Caution (both)
