GK-003 — Piston Engine: Four-Stroke Cycle

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · PPL Written Exam

The Four Strokes
Stroke 1
INTAKE
Piston moves DOWN, intake valve opens. Fuel-air mixture drawn into cylinder.
Intake: OPEN
Exhaust: CLOSED
Stroke 2
COMPRESSION
Piston moves UP, both valves closed. Mixture compressed to ~1/8 original volume.
Intake: CLOSED
Exhaust: CLOSED
Stroke 3
POWER
Spark ignites mixture. Expanding gases force piston DOWN — the only stroke producing power.
Intake: CLOSED
Exhaust: CLOSED
Stroke 4
EXHAUST
Piston moves UP, exhaust valve opens. Burnt gases expelled from cylinder.
Intake: CLOSED
Exhaust: OPEN
Engine Components Reference
ComponentFunctionNote
CarburetorMixes fuel and air in correct ratioSusceptible to icing at 0–21°C with high humidity
Magnetos (×2)Generate high-voltage spark independently of aircraft electrical systemEach magneto fires one plug per cylinder; both must work — tested during run-up
Spark Plugs (×2 per cyl)Ignite fuel-air mixture at correct timingDual plugs provide redundancy and complete combustion
CrankshaftConverts reciprocating piston motion to rotary motionDrives propeller and accessories
CamshaftControls opening/closing timing of intake and exhaust valvesDriven by crankshaft at half speed
Oil SystemLubricates, cools, and cleans engine componentsCheck oil level & pressure before and during flight
Exam Tip: The four strokes in order — Intake, Compression, Power, Exhaust — can be remembered as "I Can't Produce Exhaust" or "ICPE". Only the Power stroke generates force; the other three are driven by engine momentum (flywheel). A 4-cylinder engine fires one power stroke every 180° of crankshaft rotation.
