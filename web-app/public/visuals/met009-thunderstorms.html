MET-009 — Thunderstorms

Transport Canada · TP 12880E · AIM MET 2.8 · Avoid by 20 NM

Three Ingredients for Any Thunderstorm
1
Unstable air
Steep temperature lapse rate
2
Moisture
High humidity through the column
3
Lifting trigger
Surface heating, front, terrain, or convergence
Three-Stage Lifecycle
Every Hazard in One Cell
Severe turbulence — updraft/downdraft shear inside and around the cell.
Hail — can be ejected from anvil up to 20 NM downwind.
Lightning — damage to avionics, blind crew temporarily.
Severe icing — supercooled water in updraft region.
Wind shear / microburst — especially on approach/departure.
Tornadoes — associated with severe supercells.
Heavy rain — IMC, engine flame-out possible.
Static interference — P-static on radios near cells.
Two Types — Airmass vs Frontal/Squall
TypeTriggerTypical Behaviour
Airmass (single-cell)Afternoon surface heatingScattered, short-lived, avoidable by deviating.
Frontal / squall lineCold front or trough liftingOrganised line; may be unavoidable — delay or divert. Severe weather common.
Supercell / multi-cellStrong vertical wind shearLong-lived (hours), severe to extreme hazards; tornadoes possible.
EXAM HOOK
Minimum safe distance from any thunderstorm: 20 NM. Never fly under the anvil — hail and severe turbulence possible 10+ NM from the cell. The mature stage is most violent (updraft + downdraft side-by-side). A single airmass cell lasts ~60 min total; squall lines can last hours.
