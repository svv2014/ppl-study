MET-009 — Thunderstorms

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Chapter 8 · PPL Written Exam

Three Requirements for Thunderstorm Development
Requirement Description Example Source
1. Sufficient Moisture Adequate water vapour in the lower troposphere to fuel condensation and latent heat release Moist air from Gulf of Mexico, Great Lakes, ocean surfaces
2. A Lifting Mechanism Force to lift moist air to its condensation level (LCL) and beyond free convection level Surface heating, cold front, orographic lifting, low pressure
3. Instability Environmental lapse rate exceeds saturated adiabatic lapse rate — rising air accelerates Cold air aloft over warm surface; steep environmental lapse rate
Thunderstorm Lifecycle — Three Stages
Stage 1: Cumulus
Duration
~20 minutes
Air motion
Updrafts only — 3,000 to 8,000 fpm
Precipitation
None reaching surface yet (held aloft by updraft)
Cloud appearance
Building cumulus towering rapidly
Hazards
Moderate turbulence
Recognizable by
Rapidly building Cu — can grow 1,000 ft/min
Stage 2: Mature
Duration
~20-40 minutes (most intense phase)
Air motion
BOTH updrafts AND downdrafts simultaneously
Precipitation
Heavy rain, hail reaching surface
Cloud appearance
Full Cb with anvil spreading at tropopause
Hazards
ALL hazards present
Recognizable by
Anvil top, heavy rain, lightning frequency increases
Stage 3: Dissipating
Duration
~20-30 minutes
Air motion
Downdrafts dominate — cuts off updraft supply
Precipitation
Light rain, drizzle; then ending
Cloud appearance
Anvil persists; cloud base dissolves
Hazards
Still turbulence + icing
Recognizable by
Precipitation lightens; lightning decreases; anvil remains
Thunderstorm Hazard Summary
Hazard Description Stage Severity
Turbulence Updrafts up to 8,000 fpm and downdrafts simultaneously; extreme shear between them Mature (worst) Extreme
Icing All types present: rime, clear, and mixed; supercooled water from base to top All stages Severe
Lightning Every Cb produces lightning; can strike aircraft several miles from storm; damages avionics Mature (peak) Extreme
Hail Can be ejected 20+ NM from storm under the anvil; largest hail in mature stage updraft core Mature Severe
Wind Shear Outflow boundary = sudden wind shift + gust front at surface; approaches at up to 100 kt Mature / Dissipating Severe
Microbursts Intense downdraft < 4 km diameter; downdraft to 6,000 fpm; outflow to 45 kt Mature Extreme
Tornadoes Supercell Cb can produce tornadoes; rare in Canada but documented Mature (supercell) Extreme
Low-Level Wind Shear Rapid airspeed and direction changes on approach/departure; invisible hazard Mature / Dissipating Extreme on approach

Thunderstorm Avoidance Rules — Non-Negotiable

20 NM RULEClear all Cb by at least 20 NM laterally. Hail, turbulence, and lightning can extend well beyond the visible cloud boundary.
NEVER ENTERNever intentionally fly into a cumulonimbus. No aircraft certified for PPL operations is designed to safely penetrate an active Cb.
NEVER UNDERNever fly beneath a Cb. The gust front, downbursts, and heavy precipitation under a mature Cb are lethal hazards at low altitude.
ANVIL SIDEIf you must pass near a storm, go on the upwind (windward) side. The anvil and hail extend downwind. The upwind side is still hazardous but less so.
DIVERTIf you cannot safely circumnavigate, divert and wait. A storm dissipates in ~1 hour. No schedule justifies penetrating a thunderstorm.

Memory Hook — Thunderstorm Stages

CUMULUSUpdrafts only. Storm is growing — building fast. Avoid. No precipitation at surface yet.
MATUREMost dangerous stage — both updrafts AND downdrafts. All hazards active simultaneously: turbulence, icing, hail, lightning, microbursts.
DISSIPATINGDowndrafts dominate. Storm dying, but still dangerous — icing and turbulence persist. Anvil can remain hours after storm ends.
THREE NEEDSMoisture + Lift + Instability. Remove any one and no thunderstorm forms. Forecasters look for all three to predict convective activity.
