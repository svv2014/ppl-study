MET-013 — Fronts

Transport Canada · TP 12880E Chapter 8 · PPL Written Exam

Front Type Overview
Cold Front
Symbol
Blue line with solid triangles pointing in direction of movement
Movement speed
Fast: 25–50 kt (can be faster); steeper slope than warm front
What happens
Cold dense air undercuts and lifts warm moist air rapidly. Forces strong convection.
Cloud sequence (approaching)
Cu → TCu → Cb; possible squall line ahead of front
Precipitation
Heavy showers, thunderstorms; narrow band (50–100 NM wide); intense but brief
Visibility (prefrontal)
Good in clear sector; hazy near front
Turbulence
Severe — strong vertical motion; Cb possible
Icing
All types — convective cloud; all altitudes in Cb
After passage
Rapid clearing; wind shifts clockwise (veers) to NW; pressure rises quickly; visibility excellent
Warm Front
Symbol
Red line with solid semicircles pointing in direction of movement
Movement speed
Slow: 10–25 kt; shallow slope (~1:150)
What happens
Warm air overrides cold; gradual lifting over wide area produces stratiform cloud/rain
Cloud sequence (approaching)
Ci → Cs → As → Ns (hundreds of miles ahead of surface position)
Precipitation
Continuous rain or snow; wide band (300–500 NM ahead of surface front)
Visibility (prefrontal)
Poor — fog, mist, stratus common ahead of surface front
Turbulence
Light to moderate — stratiform; occasional moderate in Ns
Icing
Severe icing risk — widespread freezing rain possible; large area of icing
After passage
Gradual improvement; temperature rises; wind shifts (veers); visibility slowly improves; may remain foggy
Occluded Front
Symbol
Purple line with alternating triangles and semicircles on same side
Formation
Cold front catches warm front; warm sector lifted entirely off surface. Most complex wx.
Types
Cold occlusion (cold-type): cold air behind front colder than cold air ahead. Warm occlusion (warm-type): reverse.
Cloud/Precipitation
Combined characteristics of both cold and warm fronts; complex weather pattern
Turbulence
MOD to SEV — combined hazards
Icing
Significant icing — wide area, multiple levels
Visibility
Generally poor; extensive cloud and precipitation area
Stationary Front
Symbol
Alternating red semicircles (warm side) and blue triangles (cold side) pointing opposite directions
Movement
Little or no movement; wind nearly parallel to frontal boundary on both sides
Weather
Prolonged cloudiness and precipitation; similar to warm front but stationary for days
Duration
Can persist for days; often leads to flooding rains and persistent IFR conditions
Icing
Persistent icing — extended exposure due to slow movement
Planning
Requires long detour or delay; check GFA for extent of affected area

Memory Hook — Fronts at a Glance

COLD = FASTCold front: fast, narrow, dramatic. Violent weather, then rapid clearing. If you can wait 2 hours, it may pass. Cb and squall lines possible.
WARM = SLOWWarm front: slow, wide, persistent. Cloud sequence hundreds of miles ahead: Ci → Cs → As → Ns. Freezing rain hazard. Cannot wait it out easily.
CLOUD SEQUENCECi → Cs (halo!) → As → Ns = warm front approach. Halo around sun/moon means Cs = front within 24–36 hours. Classic exam question.
WIND AFTERWind veers (clockwise: S to W to NW) after both cold and warm front passage in Northern Hemisphere. Pressure rises after cold front.
