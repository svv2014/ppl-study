NAV-007 — Fuel Planning

Transport Canada · CARs 602.88 · TP 12880E Ch.9 · PPL Written Exam

Fuel Calculation — Step by Step
1

Calculate Time En Route

Use Ground Speed (from E6B wind solution) and total distance.

Time (hr) = Distance (NM) ÷ Ground Speed (kt)
2

Calculate En Route Fuel

Multiply time by fuel burn rate from POH. Fuel burn is given in litres/hr or USG/hr.

Fuel (L) = Burn Rate (L/hr) × Time (hr)
3

Add Statutory Reserve (CARs 602.88)

Day VFR: 30 min reserve at cruise burn. Night VFR: 45 min reserve. Reserve is calculated at normal cruise power.

Reserve = Burn Rate × 0.5 hr (day) or 0.75 hr (night)
4

Total Fuel Required

Sum all legs and reserve. Verify against aircraft's usable fuel capacity.

Total = taxi + en route (all legs) + reserve
5

Verify Usable Fuel ≥ Total Required

Check POH for usable fuel (total capacity minus unusable). If insufficient, plan a fuel stop. Never depart with less fuel than total required.

Fuel Unit Conversions

  • 1 US gallon (USG) = 3.785 litres
  • 1 Imperial gallon = 4.546 litres
  • AVGAS 100LL density: 0.72 kg/L (6.0 lb/USG)
  • POH usually gives burn in USG/hr — convert to litres for fuel truck

Usable vs Total Fuel

  • Total capacity — stamped on fuel cap or in POH specs
  • Unusable fuel — trapped in lines/sump; cannot be used in flight
  • Usable fuel = Total − Unusable; this is your planning number
  • Endurance = Usable fuel ÷ Burn rate (includes reserve)

Exam Memory Hooks

GS NOT TASFuel planning uses Ground Speed to find time, not TAS. A headwind means more time en route, so more fuel consumed.
RESERVEDay VFR = 30 min, Night VFR = 45 min reserve. This is from CARs 602.88. The reserve must be at cruise power burn, not economy.
ENDURANCEEndurance (how long you can fly) = Usable Fuel ÷ Burn Rate. Subtract the 30-min reserve to find usable flight time to empty.
