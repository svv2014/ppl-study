Key Formulas

Fuel Required

L = FF × T
Fuel Flow (L/hr) × Time (hrs)

Example: FF = 35 L/hr, trip = 2.0 hr
Trip fuel = 35 × 2.0 = 70 L

Fuel flow is found in the aircraft POH/AFM performance tables at the planned cruise power setting and altitude.

VFR Reserve Requirement

30 min
CAR 602.88 — VFR Day minimum

Reserve fuel = FF × 0.5 hr
Example: FF = 35 L/hr
Reserve = 35 × 0.5 = 17.5 L ≈ 18 L

Reserve is calculated at normal cruise power, not full throttle.

Fuel Planning Steps
Step Action Source / Method
1 Determine total trip time (ETE) Nav log — sum all leg ETEs
2 Calculate trip fuel: FF × trip time POH cruise performance table
3 Add 30-min VFR reserve: FF × 0.5 hr CAR 602.88 requirement
4 Add taxi & run-up fuel (typically 3–5 L) Standard/operator practice
5 Total = trip + reserve + taxi; compare to usable fuel Aircraft fuel gauges / POH capacity

Memory Hooks

Key Concepts to Remember

30 MIN VFR day minimum reserve is 30 minutes at normal cruise per CAR 602.88. Night VFR requires 45 minutes. These are legal minimums — plan to land with more.
LITRES Canadian aircraft fuel is measured in litres (L) for Avgas in training aircraft. Some POHs use US gallons or pounds — always check and convert consistently before planning.
CHECK Always verify total required vs. usable fuel capacity. "Tabs full" doesn't mean full tanks — check the POH for usable fuel. Never plan to land on reserve alone.
