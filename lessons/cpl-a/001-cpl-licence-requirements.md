# Lesson CAL-001: CPL-A Licence Requirements and Privileges

**Subject:** Commercial Air Law  
**Lesson number:** 001  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Sources:** CARs Part IV, CARs 401.17, TP 12880E, TC CPL-A PTS

---

## Narration Script

Welcome to the first lesson of the CPL-A Commercial Air Law series. This lesson covers the regulatory foundation for the Commercial Pilot Licence — Aeroplane in Canada. Every CPL-A candidate should know these requirements cold before sitting the written exam.

---

### What the CPL-A Is

The Commercial Pilot Licence — Aeroplane (CPL-A) is a Transport Canada licence that authorizes a pilot to act as PIC of an aeroplane in commercial air service operations. The defining privilege it adds — over and above the PPL — is the right to carry passengers or cargo **for hire or reward**. Without a CPL-A, accepting payment for flight services is not permitted under Canadian law.

The regulatory foundation is **CARs Part IV — Personnel Licensing and Training**, specifically **CARs 401.17**, which sets out all requirements for initial issue of the CPL-A.

---

### Age and Medical Requirements

You must meet two administrative thresholds before any flight time requirements apply:

**Minimum age:** 18 years. You cannot be issued a CPL-A if you are under 18, regardless of flight experience.

**Medical certificate:** Category 1. The PPL requires a Category 3 medical. The CPL-A steps up to a Category 1 Medical Certificate, issued by a Transport Canada Civil Aviation Medical Examiner (CAME). Category 1 standards are stricter — particularly for vision, cardiovascular health, and certain neurological conditions. If you currently hold a PPL with a Category 3 medical, you must upgrade to Category 1 before the CPL-A can be issued. Source: CARs 404.04 read in conjunction with CARs 401.17.

---

### Flight Time Requirements

CARs 401.17 specifies minimum hours. These are floors, not targets — most candidates need more time to reach the required proficiency.

| Requirement | Minimum Hours |
|-------------|---------------|
| Total flight time | 200 hours |
| PIC time | 100 hours |
| PIC cross-country | 20 hours |
| Night flight (PIC, with night rating) | 5 hours |
| Instrument flight time (actual or simulated) | 5 hours |
| Instrument ground time | 10 hours |

**Total time (200 hours):** This is time logged as pilot-in-command or as dual instruction. Hours under Part IV training count fully. Military equivalencies may apply under certain conditions — but for the civilian path, 200 total is the number.

**PIC time (100 hours):** At least half your total time must be as pilot-in-command. This incentivizes building PIC time early in training rather than accumulating dual instruction hours right to the limit.

**PIC cross-country (20 hours):** Cross-country for this purpose means a flight including a landing at a point more than 25 nautical miles from the departure aerodrome. This must be PIC time — dual instruction cross-country does not count toward this 20-hour sub-requirement.

**Night and instrument:** You must already hold, or complete the requirements for, a night rating. The 5 hours instrument and 10 hours instrument ground time ensure you have basic exposure to IFR technique — even though a CPL-A alone does not authorize instrument flight.

---

### Knowledge and Skill Requirements

**Written exam:** You must pass the Transport Canada CPL-A written exam. The exam tests all commercial air law topics, advanced meteorology, advanced navigation, aerodynamics, and aircraft performance. Passing grade is 60%. You must book and pass this exam before the flight test.

**Flight test:** You must demonstrate the competencies in the **TC CPL-A Pilot Training Standards (PTS)**. The flight test is conducted by a Transport Canada-authorized pilot examiner. The PTS document lists required exercises, performance criteria, and common failure points.

**Language:** You must demonstrate adequate English or French proficiency for aviation communication. This is assessed during training and affirmed by the endorsement of your instructor.

---

### CPL-A Privileges

Once issued, a CPL-A allows you to:

- Act as PIC of an aeroplane in any operation for which an Airline Transport Pilot Licence (ATPL) is not specifically required
- Carry passengers and cargo **for hire or reward**
- Act as a flight crew member in commercial air services (e.g., charter, air taxi)
- Provide flight instruction only if you additionally hold a Flight Instructor Rating

**What the CPL-A does NOT authorize without additional ratings:**
- Instrument flight (requires an Instrument Rating)
- Multi-engine operations (requires a Multi-Engine Class Rating)
- Airline transport operations as PIC (requires an ATPL)

---

### Key Distinctions from PPL

| Requirement | PPL-A | CPL-A |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| Minimum age | 17 | 18 |
| Medical | Category 3 | Category 1 |
| Total flight time | 45 hours | 200 hours |
| Commercial hire privilege | No | Yes |
| Instrument hours required | No | Yes (5 + 10 ground) |

The medical upgrade is the most common overlooked requirement. Start your Category 1 medical exam early — if a condition is borderline, the assessment process can take weeks.

---

## Key Points

1. **CARs 401.17** is the governing section — know it by reference
2. Minimum age: **18**, minimum medical: **Category 1**
3. 200 total hours, 100 PIC, 20 PIC cross-country, 5 night, 5 instrument, 10 instrument ground
4. Written exam first, then flight test
5. The core new privilege: carrying passengers/cargo **for hire or reward**
