# Lesson CAL-005: Commercial VFR and Special Operations

**Subject:** Commercial Air Law  
**Lesson number:** 005  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Sources:** CARs 602.115, 602.117, 602.120, CARs 703.29, TP 12880E

---

## Narration Script

Lesson CAL-005 covers VFR weather rules as they apply specifically to commercial operations, plus two special VFR concepts: VFR OTT (Over The Top) and Special VFR (SVFR). Commercial VFR rules are stricter than the private pilot minimums you learned for PPL-A — and the exam will test whether you know the difference.

---

### Baseline VFR Minimums (Review)

From PPL-A, you know the standard VFR minimums under CARs 602.115:

**Controlled airspace (Class C, D, E) — Day:**
- 3 statute miles flight visibility
- 500 feet below / 1,000 feet above / 2,000 feet horizontal from cloud

**Class G — Day, at and below 1,000 feet AGL:**
- 2 statute miles visibility
- Clear of cloud

These minimums are floors for *all* VFR flight. Commercial operations often impose **higher minimums** than these.

---

### Commercial Night VFR (CARs 703.29)

Operating commercially at night is higher risk — even with a night rating, the absence of visual horizon and terrain references means weather minimums must be stricter.

Under **CARs 703.29**, a CARs 703 (Air Taxi) operator conducting VFR night flights must apply:

- **Flight visibility:** 5 statute miles (versus 3 miles day minimum)
- **Ceiling:** At least 1,000 feet

These are the **operator minimums** — individual operators may set even higher company minimums in their Operations Manual, but they cannot go below the CARs 703.29 floor.

The practical implication: a VFR night charter that departs in acceptable conditions may not be able to continue if the ceiling drops below 1,000 feet at the destination. Dispatch planning must account for this.

---

### VFR Over The Top (CARs 602.117)

VFR OTT means flying VFR above a cloud layer. The flight is entirely under VFR rules — no instrument rating, no IFR equipment requirement, no ATC IFR clearance.

**Requirements under CARs 602.117:**
- The pilot must have current weather information confirming that VMC will exist at the planned destination and alternates during the planned arrival period
- The pilot must be able to descend through the cloud layer to the destination (or alternate) in VMC — not through IFR conditions
- The aircraft must be equipped for VFR flight (standard VFR equipment)

**The risk:** VFR OTT can become a trap. If destination weather deteriorates and you cannot descend in VMC, you are trapped above cloud with no IFR capability. Transport Canada strongly recommends having an alternate route or alternate that can be reached in VMC before committing to VFR OTT.

VFR OTT is not available at night. At night, if you are above cloud, you need an Instrument Rating and appropriate clearance.

---

### Special VFR (CARs 602.120)

Special VFR (SVFR) allows a VFR aircraft to operate within a **control zone** when weather is below standard VFR minimums — but only if ATC issues an SVFR clearance and the visibility is at least 1 statute mile (in some contexts, as defined by the ATC unit).

**Day SVFR:**
- No instrument rating required
- Need ATC clearance
- Must remain clear of cloud
- Minimum visibility as directed by ATC

**Night SVFR:**
- Requires an **Instrument Rating**
- Aircraft must be IFR-equipped
- ATC clearance required

**Important:** SVFR is only available within a **control zone** — the small cylinder of controlled airspace around a towered airport. It is not a blank authorization to fly below VFR minimums anywhere; it is a local dispensation granted by ATC for arrivals and departures at a specific aerodrome when weather is marginal.

---

### Operator-Imposed Minimums

Air operators under CARs Part VII are permitted — and often required — to set weather minimums that are **higher** than the CARs regulatory floors. These appear in the operator's Operations Manual.

Example: A 703 operator might specify company minimums of:
- Day: 5 miles visibility and 1,500 feet ceiling (higher than the 3-mile CARs minimum)
- Night: 5 miles and 1,500 feet (higher than 703.29's 1,000-foot ceiling minimum)

As a line pilot, you must comply with **whichever is more restrictive** — the CARs minimum or the company minimum. You cannot use the CARs minimum as justification to depart below company minimums.

---

### Decision-Making Framework for VFR Commercial Ops

Apply this hierarchy when evaluating departure and en-route decisions:

1. **Can I legally go?** — Is weather at or above CARs minimums (602.115, 703.29)?
2. **Can I legally go by company standards?** — Do I meet the Operations Manual minimums?
3. **Should I go?** — Is the trip safe given actual conditions, trends, and pilot capability?

Step 3 is a personal judgment. PIC authority includes the right to refuse a commercial flight that is legal on paper but unsafe in practice.

---

## Key Points

1. **Commercial night VFR (CARs 703.29):** 5 miles visibility, 1,000-foot ceiling minimum
2. **VFR OTT (CARs 602.117):** Legal without instrument rating if VMC confirmed at destination/alternates; not available at night
3. **Special VFR (CARs 602.120):** Control zone only; no instrument rating needed by day; instrument rating required at night
4. Company minimums may be stricter than CARs — comply with the **more restrictive** standard
5. Day VFR cloud clearance in controlled airspace: **500 below / 1,000 above / 2,000 horizontal** — CARs 602.115
