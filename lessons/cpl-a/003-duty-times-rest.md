# Lesson CAL-003: Flight Crew Duty Times and Rest Requirements

**Subject:** Commercial Air Law  
**Lesson number:** 003  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Sources:** CARs Part VII, CARs 700.14–700.17, TP 14371

---

## Narration Script

Duty times and rest requirements are a recurring topic on the CPL-A written exam — and even more important in real-world commercial operations. Fatigue is a documented cause of aviation accidents. Transport Canada's rules in CARs Part VII set hard limits that cannot be waived by the operator or the pilot.

---

### Why Duty-Time Rules Exist

Pilot fatigue degrades decision-making, reaction time, and situational awareness in ways that parallel alcohol impairment — but are often less obvious to the impaired person. CARs Part VII addresses fatigue through three mechanisms:

1. **Absolute flight-time ceilings** — hard limits on hours flown
2. **Rest requirements** — mandatory rest before new duty periods
3. **Personal fitness responsibility** — the pilot's own duty to refuse duty when unfit

---

### Maximum Flight Time Limits (CARs 700.15)

These are the cumulative ceilings — rolling totals, not reset on January 1:

| Period | Maximum Flight Time |
|--------|---------------------|
| 24 consecutive hours | Operator determined (within flight duty period limits) |
| 7 consecutive days | Operator determined |
| 90 consecutive days | 300 hours |
| 12 consecutive months | 1,200 hours |

The **300 hours / 90 days** limit prevents pilots from clustering hours into bursts. The **1,200 hours / 12-month** ceiling is absolute — exceeding it requires Transport Canada authorization, which is essentially never granted.

These limits apply regardless of how many operators you fly for. If you fly for two different air taxi companies simultaneously, your combined hours count toward the limits.

---

### Rest Requirements (CARs 700.16)

Before commencing a flight duty period, a flight crew member must have had a rest period that provides at least **8 continuous hours of sleep opportunity**. The rest period must be long enough that — after accounting for personal hygiene, meals, and transport to accommodation — the pilot can realistically get 8 hours of sleep.

This is different from simply "8 hours off duty." An operator cannot schedule a pilot with a 9-hour rest that includes 90 minutes of transport each way, leaving only 6 hours of actual sleep opportunity.

Extended rest may be required when:
- The preceding flight duty period was unusually long
- The pilot will be crossing multiple time zones
- The operator's Operations Manual specifies greater rest for certain routes

---

### Personal Fitness Responsibility (CARs 700.14)

This section is one of the most important — and most commonly tested. CARs 700.14 imposes a **personal duty** on each flight crew member to:

- Not commence a flight if their ability is impaired by fatigue, illness, injury, medication, or any other cause
- Report for duty only when fit to perform their assigned functions
- Declare themselves unfit if circumstances change between reporting and departure

The key point: **compliance with the duty-time numbers does not automatically make you fit to fly.** A pilot who has been legally on rest but spent that time moving house and sleeping only 3 hours is not fit to fly — even if the hours-of-rest box is technically checked. The pilot bears personal responsibility for this judgment.

Operators cannot pressure a pilot to fly when the pilot has declared themselves unfit. CARs protects a pilot's right to make this call without adverse employment consequences — though the practical dynamics of commercial operations mean this requires professional backbone.

---

### Operator Obligations

The air operator also has duties under CARs Part VII:

- Must not schedule a crew member beyond the flight-time limits
- Must provide adequate rest facilities at bases where overnight stays are required
- Must maintain records of flight crew duty times
- Must have a fatigue management program described in their Operations Manual

Under a **Fatigue Risk Management System (FRMS)**, operators may apply for alternative duty-time schemes if they can demonstrate equivalent safety through data. FRMS is an advanced topic beyond the CPL-A written exam scope.

---

### Key Numbers to Remember

| Limit | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Maximum flight time — 90 days | 300 hours |
| Maximum flight time — 12 months | 1,200 hours |
| Minimum rest before duty | 8 hours sleep opportunity |
| Personal responsibility section | CARs 700.14 |

---

## Key Points

1. **1,200 hours** maximum in any 12 consecutive months — CARs 700.15
2. **300 hours** maximum in any 90 consecutive days — CARs 700.15
3. Rest must provide **8 continuous hours of sleep opportunity** — not just 8 hours off — CARs 700.16
4. **The pilot bears personal responsibility** for fitness for duty under CARs 700.14
5. Complying with the numbers does not override a pilot's personal fitness obligation
