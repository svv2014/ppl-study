# Lesson GK-013: Radio Communications and Phraseology

**Section:** General Knowledge  
**Lesson number:** 013  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Source:** TP 12880E Chapter 9, AIM COM 5.0, CARs 602.132

---

## Narration Script

Radio communication is a core flying skill. Clear, concise, standardized radio calls reduce workload, prevent misunderstandings, and improve safety. This lesson covers radio equipment basics, standard phraseology, distress and urgency procedures, and the regulatory requirements for radio carriage in Canada.

---

**Radio Equipment Basics**

Most light training aircraft are equipped with **VHF (Very High Frequency) communications radios**, operating in the 118.000 to 136.975 MHz band. VHF communications are line-of-sight — the effective range is limited by the curvature of the Earth and terrain. Higher altitude = greater range.

The radio typically has:
- A **COM radio** (or two) for voice communication on ATC, approach, tower, and UNICOM/CTAF frequencies
- A **NAV radio** (VOR receiver) for navigation
- A **transponder** for secondary radar and mode C altitude reporting

---

**Standard Radio Call Structure**

Every effective radio call follows a consistent format:

**Initial call**: 
1. Who you're calling (the station)
2. Who you are (your callsign — aircraft type and registration)
3. Where you are (position and altitude)
4. What you want (request or intention)

Example: "Edmonton Ground, Cessna Golf Foxtrot Alfa Bravo, at the terminal ramp, requesting VFR departure to the south, information Tango received."

**Readback**: When ATC issues a clearance, you must read it back. ATC can then correct any error. Always read back runway assignments, hold short instructions, transponder codes, altimeter settings, and altitude assignments.

**Acknowledgement**: If you receive information only (not a clearance), acknowledge with your callsign: "Golf Foxtrot Alfa Bravo."

**Frequencies and Their Purposes**

- **Tower/Ground**: at controlled airports, separate frequencies for taxiing (ground) and takeoff/landing (tower)
- **ATIS**: automatic terminal information service; a recorded broadcast of current weather, NOTAM, active runway. Listen and include the information letter in your initial call ("with information Juliet").
- **Approach/Departure**: radar services for arriving and departing IFR and VFR aircraft at busy airports
- **UNICOM**: frequency at non-towered airports providing advisory service (weather, fuel). Operated by the FBO, not ATC.
- **CTAF/ATF/MF**: common traffic advisory frequency or mandatory frequency at uncontrolled aerodromes — used for self-announce position calls

---

**Uncontrolled Aerodrome Radio Calls**

At an uncontrolled aerodrome, pilots self-announce on the ATF/MF. Standard calls:
- **Joining the circuit**: "Cessna Golf ABC, joining right downwind, Runway 28, Buttonville."
- **Downwind**: "Cessna Golf ABC, downwind Runway 28, Buttonville."
- **Base**: "Cessna Golf ABC, base Runway 28, Buttonville."
- **Final**: "Cessna Golf ABC, final Runway 28, Buttonville."
- **Backtrack/departing**: "Cessna Golf ABC, backtracking Runway 28, Buttonville."

Include airport name in calls to prevent confusion when multiple airports share a frequency.

---

**Distress and Urgency**

**MAYDAY** — Distress (from French: m'aidez, "help me"). Grave and imminent danger:
- Repeated three times: "Mayday Mayday Mayday"
- Contents: callsign, nature of emergency, position, altitude, fuel remaining, persons on board, intentions
- Transmit on current frequency or 121.5 MHz (international distress frequency)
- ATC will provide priority handling and coordinate assistance

**PAN PAN** — Urgency. Serious situation but not immediately life-threatening (e.g., lost, low on fuel but not emergency, medical issue not incapacitating):
- Repeated three times: "Pan Pan Pan Pan Pan Pan"
- Same information content as MAYDAY

121.5 MHz is the international emergency frequency — monitored by ATC, military, and Transport Canada. ELTs (Emergency Locator Transmitters) transmit on 121.5 MHz and 406 MHz when activated.

---

**Radio Carriage Requirements — CARs 602.132**

Radio equipment is required for operations in:
- **Class B** airspace (12,500 to 18,000 ft ASL)
- **Class C** airspace (controlled, towered airports)
- **Class D** airspace (towered airports)
- **Mandatory Frequency (MF) areas** at certain uncontrolled aerodromes
- **Transponder** required in Class B, C, and certain Class D airspace

VFR flight in Class E and G airspace without a radio is legal (though inadvisable). However, any flight into controlled airspace requires radio equipment and communication.

---

## Key Points

- **Radio call order**: who you're calling → who you are → where you are → what you want
- **Readback** all clearances, runway assignments, transponder codes, and altitude instructions
- **Phonetic "fife"** for 5; **"niner"** for 9
- **MAYDAY** = grave and imminent danger; **PAN PAN** = urgency, serious but not immediate threat
- **121.5 MHz** = international emergency/distress frequency
- ATF/MF at uncontrolled aerodromes: self-announce, no ATC, other traffic monitoring
- **CARs 602.132**: radio required in Class B, C, D airspace and MF areas
