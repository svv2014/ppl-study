# Lesson GK-012: Human Factors and Pilot Decision-Making

**Section:** General Knowledge  
**Lesson number:** 012  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Source:** TP 12880E Chapter 15, Transport Canada Human Factors materials

---

## Narration Script

Human error contributes to the vast majority of aviation accidents. Understanding the physiological and psychological factors that degrade pilot performance is as important as understanding aerodynamics. This lesson covers the most common human factors hazards tested on the PPL written exam: hypoxia, carbon monoxide, hyperventilation, spatial disorientation, fatigue, hazardous attitudes, and aeronautical decision-making.

---

**Hypoxia**

The brain requires a continuous supply of oxygen. At altitude, the atmospheric pressure decreases, reducing the partial pressure of oxygen. Even though the percentage of oxygen in air remains constant (about 21%), less oxygen enters the bloodstream with each breath.

**Symptoms of hypoxia** (insidious — often undetected by the victim):
- Euphoria and false sense of well-being
- Impaired judgement and memory
- Tingling and numbness
- Blue fingernails/lips (cyanosis) — late sign
- Vision narrowing, tunnel vision
- Eventually loss of consciousness

**Onset altitude**: significant impairment typically begins above 10,000–12,000 ft for most people, but susceptibility varies widely. Fatigue, alcohol, cold, and illness lower the threshold.

**Treatment**: descend to below 10,000 ft and/or use supplemental oxygen. Above 10,000 ft on a long flight, supplemental oxygen is recommended. CARs 605.31 requires oxygen above specific altitudes for flight crew.

---

**Carbon Monoxide Poisoning**

CO is produced by incomplete combustion and is present in aircraft engine exhaust gases. Aircraft cabin heaters work by routing cabin air over the exhaust pipe through a heat exchanger. If the heat exchanger or exhaust manifold cracks, CO infiltrates the heated air entering the cabin.

**Symptoms**: headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness — similar to hypoxia. CO binds to haemoglobin much more effectively than oxygen, displacing oxygen from the blood.

**Detection**: CO is odourless and colourless. A CO detector (coloured dot or electronic meter) in the cockpit provides the only reliable warning.

**Action if suspected**: heater off, full fresh air on, open vents, 100% oxygen if available, land immediately.

---

**Hyperventilation**

Overbreathing removes too much CO2 from the blood. The body uses CO2 levels (not oxygen levels) to regulate breathing — low CO2 (hypocapnia) causes physiological effects that mimic hypoxia.

**Symptoms**: tingling in hands and feet, dizziness, vision changes, chest tightness, anxiety, possibly fainting.

**Trigger**: anxiety, stress, mild hypoxia.

**Treatment**: slow and deepen breathing deliberately. Breathing into a bag (increases inhaled CO2) speeds recovery.

**Key distinction**: hypoxia → need more oxygen; hyperventilation → need more CO2 (less breathing). They produce similar symptoms but opposite treatments.

---

**Spatial Disorientation**

The vestibular system (inner ear) detects rotation and acceleration. Without visual reference to the horizon, the vestibular system can provide false sensations:

- **The leans**: after a prolonged banked turn, returning to wings-level feels like banking in the opposite direction. The pilot leans against the perceived bank.
- **Graveyard spiral**: the pilot becomes accustomed to a spiral turn and, if it steepens, a pull-back on the stick tightens the spiral. The instrument indications look like a climb, not a bank.
- **Somatogravic illusion**: rapid acceleration creates the sensation of pitching nose-up; the pilot pitches nose down — into terrain.

**Treatment**: rely on flight instruments, not body sensations. Trust the AI and the ASI. VFR pilots must never allow themselves to enter IMC — spatial disorientation is virtually guaranteed and typically fatal.

---

**Fatigue**

Fatigue impairs judgement, reaction time, memory, and decision-making. Even mild fatigue has measurable effects on pilot performance.

**Types**: 
- Acute fatigue: from short-term sleep loss or overwork; reversible with rest
- Chronic fatigue: prolonged sleep debt; not resolved with a single good night's sleep

**Signs**: impaired concentration, slow reactions, mood changes, errors in routine tasks.

CARs 700.20 (commercial) and advisory material address flight time limitations. As a private pilot, you must self-regulate.

---

**Hazardous Attitudes (Transport Canada identifies five)**

1. **Anti-authority** ("Don't tell me what to do") — disregard for rules; antidote: *follow the rules*
2. **Impulsivity** ("Do something — NOW!") — acting without thinking; antidote: *not so fast, think first*
3. **Invulnerability** ("It won't happen to me") — underestimating risk; antidote: *it could happen to me*
4. **Macho** ("I can do it") — showing off, taking unnecessary risks; antidote: *taking chances is foolish*
5. **Resignation** ("What's the use?") — giving up; antidote: *I'm not helpless, I can make a difference*

---

**Aeronautical Decision-Making (ADM)**

A structured decision-making process:

1. **Identify the hazard or problem**
2. **Assess the risk** (likelihood × severity)
3. **Consider options** and their consequences
4. **Choose the best option**
5. **Evaluate the outcome**

The **DECIDE model**: Detect, Estimate, Choose, Identify, Do, Evaluate.

**IMSAFE checklist** (self-assessment before flight): Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion.

**Alcohol**: CARs 602.03 requires at least 8 hours between alcohol consumption and acting as crew member, and the blood alcohol concentration must be below 0.04% (lower than the legal driving limit of 0.08%). These are minimums — "8 hours from bottle to throttle" may be insufficient after heavy drinking.

---

## Key Points

- **Hypoxia**: insufficient oxygen; impairs judgement before awareness; treat with descent/O₂
- **CO poisoning**: from cracked heat exchanger; heater off, fresh air, land immediately
- **Hyperventilation**: too little CO₂ from overbreathing; treat by slowing breathing
- **Spatial disorientation**: trust instruments, never trust body sensations in IMC
- **Fatigue**: impairs all cognitive functions; do not fly when significantly fatigued
- **5 hazardous attitudes**: anti-authority, impulsivity, invulnerability, macho, resignation
- **IMSAFE**: Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion
- **Alcohol**: CARs 602.03 — 8 hours minimum, BAC below 0.04%
