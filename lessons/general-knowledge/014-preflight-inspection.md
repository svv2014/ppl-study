# Lesson GK-014: Pre-Flight Inspection and Maintenance

**Section:** General Knowledge  
**Lesson number:** 014  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Source:** CARs Part V, CARs 605.03, TP 12880E Chapter 14

---

## Narration Script

Airworthiness is the legal and practical basis for safe flight. As pilot-in-command, you are responsible for ensuring the aircraft is airworthy before every flight. This lesson covers what airworthiness means under Canadian regulations, what documents must be on board, the maintenance inspection system, and Airworthiness Directives.

---

**What is Airworthiness?**

An aircraft is airworthy when it:
1. Conforms to its type design (the approved design as certificated by Transport Canada)
2. Is in a condition for safe operation

Both conditions must be met simultaneously. An aircraft that conforms to its type design but has a cracked windshield or an inoperative required instrument is not airworthy.

The **Certificate of Airworthiness (C of A)** confirms that the aircraft was in an airworthy condition when the certificate was issued. It does not expire on a calendar date — it remains valid as long as the aircraft is properly maintained under Canadian airworthiness requirements. If the aircraft is involved in an accident, modified outside approved data, or allowed to exceed maintenance intervals, the C of A becomes invalid.

---

**PIC Airworthiness Responsibility**

**CARs 602.02** places responsibility for airworthiness on the Pilot-in-Command. Before every flight, the PIC must:

1. Complete a pre-flight inspection in accordance with the POH/AFM
2. Ensure all required maintenance has been performed (no outstanding ADs or time-expired items)
3. Confirm all required documents are on board
4. Assess any known defects — use the MEL (Minimum Equipment List) if one is approved, or confirm the defect does not affect airworthiness

---

**Required On-Board Documents (CARs 605.03)**

The following must be carried on board:

1. **Certificate of Airworthiness** (or Special Flight Permit)
2. **Certificate of Registration** — proof the aircraft is registered in Canada with Transport Canada
3. **Radio Station Licence** — required if the aircraft is equipped with a radio (issued by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, formerly Industry Canada)
4. **Journey Log (or aircraft journey log)** — a flight-by-flight record of aircraft use; records total flight time, defects, fuel and oil quantities

Additionally, the **Pilot Operating Handbook (POH) or AFM** (Approved Flight Manual) must be accessible to the crew during flight.

**The pilot's own documents** (licence, medical certificate, ratings) are personal items carried by the pilot — they are not "aircraft documents" under CARs 605.03, but they must also be present.

---

**Maintenance System — Canadian Requirements**

Canadian aircraft maintenance is overseen by **Transport Canada** under CARs Part V. Maintenance must be performed or supervised by an **Aircraft Maintenance Engineer (AME)** with the appropriate licence endorsements for the aircraft type.

**Maintenance Intervals:**

- **Annual inspection** (or equivalent approved inspection): required at intervals not to exceed 12 months for most aircraft not used for hire. The AME inspects the entire aircraft and signs a **Maintenance Release** in the journey log, certifying the aircraft is airworthy.

- **100-hour inspection**: required every 100 flight hours for aircraft used for hire or reward (air taxi, flight training). This is in addition to the annual requirement. The 100-hour interval may be exceeded by up to 10 hours to ferry the aircraft to a maintenance facility, but this time must be made up in the next 100-hour interval.

- **Progressive inspection program**: an alternative to the 100-hour/annual system, dividing inspections into smaller phases performed more frequently. Used by larger operations.

---

**Airworthiness Directives (ADs)**

An AD is a legally mandatory action issued by Transport Canada (or by a foreign authority, incorporated into Canadian requirements for foreign-manufactured aircraft) in response to an unsafe condition found in:
- An aircraft type design
- An engine or propeller
- An appliance (instrument, avionics, component)

ADs specify:
- **What must be done**: inspection, repair, replacement, modification
- **When it must be done**: before next flight, within X hours, within X calendar days, or at each inspection interval

**Non-compliance is illegal**. Flying an aircraft with an outstanding AD is a violation of CARs and renders the aircraft unairworthy. The AME tracks ADs as part of the maintenance release process; the PIC is also responsible for being aware of AD status.

---

**Defects and Minimum Equipment Lists (MEL)**

If a pilot discovers a defect before or during flight:

1. **Before flight**: if the defect affects airworthiness or a required instrument/equipment item, the pilot must not fly unless the defect is rectified or addressed under an approved MEL.

2. **In flight**: if a defect develops, the PIC must decide whether to continue flight or divert, using the aircraft's POH emergency/abnormal procedures.

3. **Recording**: defects must be recorded in the aircraft journey log. The AME will sign off the defect as either rectified or deferred (if MEL allows).

**INOP placard**: if a non-required instrument is found inoperative and deferred under MEL, it must be placarded "INOPERATIVE."

---

**Special Flight Permits**

A Special Flight Permit (SFP — also called a ferry permit) allows an aircraft to fly that would not otherwise meet airworthiness requirements — for example, to fly to a maintenance facility for repair, or for export after sale. It is issued by Transport Canada for a specific flight or limited number of flights.

---

**Summary of Key CARs**

- **CARs 602.02**: PIC responsible for airworthiness
- **CARs 605.03**: required documents on board
- **CARs 625 Appendix B**: inspection schedules (annual, 100-hour)
- **CARs Part V**: overarching airworthiness framework
- **CARs 571**: maintenance, repair, and modification

---

## Key Points

- **C of A** does not expire by date; valid while aircraft is properly maintained
- **PIC responsibility** (CARs 602.02): ensure airworthy before every flight
- **Required documents on board**: C of A, Registration, Radio Station Licence, Journey Log
- **Annual inspection**: required every 12 months (non-hire aircraft)
- **100-hour inspection**: required every 100 flight hours when used for hire/training
- **ADs are mandatory**: must be complied with in the specified timeframe; non-compliance is illegal
- Defects must be recorded in the journey log; PIC must not fly with unresolved airworthiness-affecting defects
