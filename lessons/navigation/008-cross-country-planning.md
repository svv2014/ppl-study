# Lesson NAV-008: Cross-Country Planning

**Section:** Navigation  
**Lesson number:** 008  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Source:** TP 12880E Chapter 9, CARs 602.73, CARs 602.34, CARs 602.88, AIM RAC 3.0

## Narration Script

Welcome to Lesson NAV-008. Cross-country flight planning brings together everything you've learned so far — charts, headings, wind correction, dead reckoning, and time-speed-distance — into one complete pre-flight workflow. This lesson walks through the complete planning process that a Canadian VFR pilot should follow before any cross-country flight, and highlights the regulatory requirements that govern fuel, altitude, and flight plans.

---

**Why Plan Before You Fly?**

Good pre-flight planning reduces workload in the air. When you've already calculated your headings, estimated your fuel, and identified your checkpoints on the ground, you can spend time in the cockpit flying the aircraft and looking outside — instead of fumbling with charts and doing mental arithmetic while also managing traffic and radio calls.

The Transport Canada written exam tests whether you know the planning process. Your actual flight test will test whether you can execute it. They are equally important.

---

**Step 1: Study the Route**

Open your VFR Navigation Chart (VNC) and identify:

- **Departure aerodrome** — note its CTAF or tower frequency
- **Destination aerodrome** — note its frequency and elevation
- **Alternate aerodrome** (optional for VFR but wise to identify)
- **Route** — draw a straight line or a series of legs that avoid controlled airspace you don't need
- **Terrain** — note the MEFs along your route; your cruise altitude must be well above these
- **Airspace** — identify any Class B, C, or D airspace along the route; check if you need ATC clearance to enter

---

**Step 2: Select Cruise Altitude**

VFR cruise altitudes in Canada are regulated by **CARs 602.34** and use the following scheme between 3,000 feet AGL and 14,000 feet ASL:

| Magnetic Track | VFR Cruising Altitude |
|---------------|----------------------|
| 000° to 179° (northbound) | Odd thousands + 500 ft (3,500 / 5,500 / 7,500 / 9,500…) |
| 180° to 359° (southbound) | Even thousands + 500 ft (2,500 / 4,500 / 6,500 / 8,500…) |

The "+500 feet" keeps VFR aircraft 500 feet above IFR cruising altitudes (which use plain thousands: 3,000, 5,000, 7,000 etc.). This vertical separation between VFR and IFR is one of the core elements of the Canadian airspace system.

Your selected altitude must also satisfy the minimum VFR clearance above terrain:
- 1,000 feet above the highest obstacle within 5 NM of your track in non-built-up areas
- 2,000 feet above the highest obstacle when over a built-up area

In practice, choosing an altitude well above the relevant MEF provides the required clearance.

---

**Step 3: Measure the Route**

Using a navigation plotter on the VNC:

1. Measure the **true track** of each leg (in degrees True)
2. Measure the **distance** of each leg (in NM)
3. Note the **variation** at each leg (from isogonic lines)

If your route changes direction significantly mid-flight (e.g., a dogleg to avoid airspace), split it into multiple legs and do the calculations for each separately.

---

**Step 4: Get Wind and Weather**

Before calculating headings and ground speeds, you need:

- **Winds Aloft forecast** — from NAV CANADA's flight planning tools (1800wxbrief.com or the CFPS — Canadian Flight Planning and Management System)
- **METARs** — current weather at your departure, en route alternates, and destination
- **TAFs** — forecast weather for arrival time at destination
- **GFA (Graphic Area Forecast)** — the big picture: icing, turbulence, cloud cover across the region
- **NOTAMs** — temporary airspace, closed runways, unserviceable navaids, parachute activities, TFRs

No flight should depart without checking all of these.

---

**Step 5: Calculate Headings and Ground Speeds (Fill the PLOG)**

For each leg, use the E6B to solve the wind triangle:
1. Enter wind direction and speed (from Winds Aloft)
2. Enter True Airspeed (from aircraft performance chart at cruise altitude)
3. Enter True Track
4. Read out: Wind Correction Angle, True Heading, and Ground Speed

Then apply variation and deviation to get the compass heading to fly. Enter all values in your navigation log (PLOG).

---

**Step 6: Fuel Planning**

Fuel calculations must account for:

- **Enroute fuel** — total time × fuel burn rate
- **Taxi/runup allowance** — typically 1–2 gallons
- **Contingency** — for weather deviations or ATC delays
- **Reserve fuel** — **CARs 602.88** requires:
  - Day VFR: 30 minutes at normal cruise speed
  - Night VFR: 45 minutes at normal cruise speed

Total fuel = (enroute time + 0.5 hour reserve) × burn rate  

Confirm the aircraft has at least this quantity as usable fuel before departure.

---

**Step 7: Filing a VFR Flight Plan**

A VFR flight plan is **mandatory (CARs 602.73)** when:
- Flying over water more than 25 NM from shore
- Flying over sparsely settled areas where a forced landing would be hazardous

A flight plan is strongly recommended for any cross-country — it is the primary mechanism for activating search and rescue if you don't arrive.

The VFR flight plan is filed with NAV CANADA via phone (1-866-WXBRIEF) or online through the flight planning system. Key fields include: departure and destination, route, planned altitude, TAS, estimated time en route, fuel endurance, number of persons on board, and aircraft description.

You must **close the flight plan** after landing by calling NAV CANADA or having the destination airport do it. If the flight plan is not closed within 30 minutes of the filed ETA, SAR alerting begins.

---

**Step 8: Pre-Departure Checks**

Before engine start:
- Review the PLOG and confirm all legs are planned
- Brief yourself on the departure procedure and first checkpoint
- Confirm airspace and altitudes
- Check fuel quantities match your plan
- File the flight plan (if required or desired)

---

**Completing the PLOG In-Flight**

As you pass each checkpoint, note the **actual time** and compare to your ETA. If early or late, calculate your actual ground speed and revise subsequent ETAs:

**Revised GS = Leg distance ÷ Actual leg time**

Update fuel remaining estimates if the GS differs significantly from plan.

---

## Quick Reference: Cross-Country Planning Steps

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Study the VNC: route, terrain, airspace |
| 2 | Select cruise altitude (CARs 602.34: ±500 ft rule) |
| 3 | Measure true track and distance per leg |
| 4 | Get weather: METARs, TAF, GFA, Winds Aloft, NOTAMs |
| 5 | E6B: solve wind triangle → heading and GS per leg |
| 6 | Fuel planning: enroute + 30 min reserve (day VFR) |
| 7 | File flight plan if required (CARs 602.73) |
| 8 | Depart; time checkpoints; update ETAs in-flight |

**VFR Cruising Altitudes (CARs 602.34):**

| Track | Altitude |
|-------|---------|
| 000°–179° (N) | Odd × 1000 + 500 ft |
| 180°–359° (S) | Even × 1000 + 500 ft |

**Fuel reserves (CARs 602.88):** Day VFR = 30 min; Night VFR = 45 min
