# Lesson MET-012: GFA — Graphical Forecast for Aviation

**Section:** Meteorology  
**Lesson number:** 012  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Source:** TP 12880E Chapter 8, AIM MET 2.4

---

## Narration Script

When planning a cross-country flight, METARs and TAFs give you point-in-time snapshots of specific airports. But what about the en route environment — the 200 nautical miles between your departure and destination? That's where the GFA comes in. The Graphical Forecast for Aviation is Canada's tool for seeing the big picture across a region: cloud layers, precipitation, icing, turbulence, and the freezing level — all on a single chart.

---

**What Is the GFA**

The Graphical Forecast for Aviation (GFA) is a set of weather forecast charts produced by **Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC)**. It replaced the older Area Forecast (FA) text product and provides the same information in a more intuitive graphical format.

Key facts:
- Issued **four times daily**: 0000, 0600, 1200, 1800 UTC
- Valid for **up to 24 hours** from issuance, in **6-hour valid-time snapshots**
- Covers all of Canada in regional panels
- Available through the NAV CANADA aviation weather website (aviation weather.gc.ca)

The GFA consists of **two chart types**:

1. **CLOUDS AND WEATHER (CLD/WX)** chart
2. **ICING, TURBULENCE AND FREEZING LEVEL (ICG/TURB/FZLVL)** chart

For a complete picture of the en route weather environment, you need BOTH charts.

---

**CLOUDS AND WEATHER Chart**

This chart depicts:

**Cloud layers** — Areas of cloud are outlined with contour lines. Inside each contour, the cloud is annotated with:
- Bottom altitude (base) in hundreds of feet ASL
- Top altitude in hundreds of feet ASL
- Coverage (SCT, BKN, OVC)

Example annotation: `SCT BKN 030-060` = scattered to broken cloud from 3,000 feet to 6,000 feet ASL.

**Ceilings** — Areas where the lowest layer is BKN or OVC at or below 3,000 feet are highlighted or annotated separately because they represent IMC-level ceilings.

**Precipitation** — Areas of precipitation are outlined and annotated with type:
- `RA` — rain
- `SN` — snow
- `FZRA` — freezing rain (CRITICAL — avoid this area unless FIKI equipped)
- `TS` — thunderstorm area (with CB symbols)
- Intensity: `-` = light, moderate unmarked, `+` = heavy

**Visibility** — Areas where visibility is forecast below 3 SM are often noted; values below 1 SM may be annotated.

**Mountain obscurement** — Areas where mountains are forecast to be obscured by cloud are important for VFR cross-country planning in terrain.

---

**Icing, Turbulence, and Freezing Level Chart**

**Freezing level** — Contour lines show where the 0°C isotherm is forecast (in feet ASL). Where multiple freezing levels exist (due to temperature inversions), both are annotated. Below the freezing level, icing is not possible due to temperatures above 0°C. Above it, structural icing in cloud is possible.

**Icing areas** — Regions of forecast icing are outlined. The annotations include:
- Intensity: `LGT` (light), `MOD` (moderate), `SEV` (severe)
- Altitude range: bottom to top in feet ASL
- Icing type if known: `RIME`, `CLR` (clear), `MXD` (mixed)

Example: `MOD RIME ICG 060-120` = moderate rime icing from 6,000 to 12,000 feet ASL.

**Turbulence areas** — Regions of forecast turbulence are outlined with:
- Intensity: `LGT`, `MOD`, `SEV`
- Altitude range
- Cause: `LLWS` (low-level wind shear), `MTNW` (mountain wave), `CB` (convective), `CAT` (clear air turbulence)

---

**How to Use the GFA for Flight Planning**

Step-by-step GFA review for a cross-country:

1. **Select the valid time** closest to your planned flight time. If your departure is at 1500Z and GFA charts are valid at 1200Z and 1800Z, use both and interpolate.

2. **Review CLOUDS AND WEATHER:**
   - Are any areas of the route forecast to have ceilings below your VFR minimums?
   - Is precipitation forecast en route? What type? Is FZRA present anywhere?
   - Are there TS areas (thunderstorms) in or near your route?

3. **Review ICING, TURBULENCE AND FREEZING LEVEL:**
   - Where is the freezing level along your planned altitude?
   - Are icing areas forecast along your route at your planned altitude? If so, what intensity?
   - Are turbulence areas — especially mountain wave (MTNW) or LLWS — present?

4. **Cross-reference with METARs and TAFs:** The GFA provides area context; METARs and TAFs provide specific aerodrome conditions for departure, destination, and alternates.

5. **Look for trends:** Compare the 1200Z and 1800Z valid-time charts to see whether conditions are improving or deteriorating.

---

**Limitations of the GFA**

- The GFA shows forecast snapshots in time — actual conditions vary continuously
- Resolution is regional — local effects (valley fog, orographic cloud) may not appear
- Thunderstorm convection timing and location have inherent forecast uncertainty
- Always supplement with PIREPs for real-time reports from pilots in the area
- The GFA is not a substitute for a full weather briefing from a Flight Information Centre (FIC)

---

**GFA in the Context of a Full Weather Briefing**

Under CARs 602.71, a pilot-in-command must obtain available weather information before a VFR flight. A complete pre-flight weather briefing includes:
- METARs and SPECIs for departure, en route, and destination
- TAFs for departure and destination
- GFA (CLD/WX and ICG/TURB/FZLVL) for the route
- SIGMETs and AIRMETs for the route
- PIREPs for the area
- NOTAMs (including weather-related NOTAMs)

The GFA is one component of this briefing, not the whole story.

---

## Key Points

- GFA issued by **ECCC**; four times daily; valid up to **24 hours** in **6-hour** snapshots
- Two chart types: **CLOUDS AND WEATHER** and **ICING/TURBULENCE/FREEZING LEVEL** — use both
- **Freezing level** contours = the 0°C altitude; icing possible above this level in cloud
- Icing annotated as LGT/MOD/SEV with altitude range and type (RIME, CLR, MXD)
- **FZRA** on CLD/WX chart = freezing rain area — avoid without FIKI equipment
- GFA provides regional context; supplement with METARs, TAFs, SIGMETs, and PIREPs
- CARs 602.71 requires weather briefing before VFR flight
