# Lesson AL-017: Low-Level Flight Rules — 500 ft Rule, Congested Areas, Noise Abatement

**Section:** Air Law  
**Lesson number:** 017  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Source:** CARs 602.13, CARs 602.14, CARs 602.15, AIM AIR 1.0, TP 12880E Chapter 10

---

## Narration Script

Welcome to Lesson AL-017. This lesson covers the rules that govern how low you can fly — and why. Low-level flight rules are one of the most commonly tested Air Law topics, because the altitude minimums depend on what is below you, not just a universal number.

---

### The General Rule — CARs 602.13

CARs 602.13 establishes the overarching principle: **no pilot shall operate an aircraft at an altitude that would prevent a safe emergency landing in the event of engine failure.**

This is not a number — it is a principle. It means you must always maintain enough altitude to glide clear of hazards below you if the engine quits. Over open, flat terrain with no obstacles and no people, this might theoretically allow very low flight. In practice, 500 feet AGL is treated as the conventional general-purpose minimum over open terrain because it provides a reasonable glide margin.

The key takeaway: 602.13 is the backstop rule that applies everywhere. The specific rules in 602.14 layer additional minimums on top of it for specific scenarios.

---

### Flight Over Congested Areas — CARs 602.14

CARs 602.14 establishes a hard numerical minimum for flight over **built-up areas** and **open-air assemblies of persons:**

**Minimum altitude: 1,000 feet above the highest obstacle within 2,000 feet horizontally of the aircraft.**

Let's break that down:

- You draw a circle of 2,000 feet radius around your aircraft's ground track.
- You find the highest obstacle within that circle — a building, tower, antenna, or tree.
- You must be at least 1,000 feet **above** that obstacle.

This rule applies to:
- Any **city, town, or settled area** (built-up area)
- Any **open-air assembly of persons** — a crowd, outdoor concert, sports event, fair

**Why 2,000 feet and 1,000 feet?** The geometry gives you a glide gradient that would let you clear the congested area in most engine failure scenarios. It is not a guarantee — it is a regulatory minimum.

**Memory hook:** 1,000 feet above, 2,000 feet around. Over cities and crowds.

---

### Flight Over Open Terrain — Practical Minimums

Over terrain that is not a built-up area and has no assembly of persons, the rule reverts to CARs 602.13 — the emergency-landing principle. Transport Canada and aviation training sources commonly state the practical minimum as **500 feet AGL** over open terrain to provide a reasonable emergency-landing capability.

On the exam, if you see a question about the minimum altitude over open farmland or rural terrain, the answer is typically **500 feet AGL** as the conventional minimum, or a reference to the emergency-landing principle of CARs 602.13.

---

### Aircraft at the Surface — Restrictions

There is an additional restriction under CARs 602.14 for operations at very low altitude: no aircraft may create a hazard to persons or property on the surface. Even in open terrain, buzzing a farmhouse at 100 feet is not legal if it endangers the occupants.

Special exemptions exist for:
- Agricultural operations (crop dusting) — separate authorization required
- Survey flights
- Search and rescue operations
- Flights authorized by a Special Flight Operations Certificate (SFOC)

---

### Formation Flying and Low-Level Operations

Low-level formation flying requires specific authorization. Aerobatic displays at low altitude near built-up areas are prohibited without an aerobatic zone designation or air show authorization. These are handled by Special Flight Operations Certificates issued under CARs Part IX.

---

### Noise Abatement Procedures

**What they are:** Many airports publish voluntary noise abatement procedures in the Canada Flight Supplement (CFS). These procedures specify preferred runways, departure headings, reduced-power segments, and circuit altitude adjustments designed to direct aircraft away from noise-sensitive residential areas.

**Are they mandatory?** No. Noise abatement procedures published in the CFS are advisory — voluntary recommendations from the airport operator. They are not regulations. You will see the phrase "noise abatement procedures recommended" or similar language.

**However:** The regulatory altitude minimums under CARs 602.13 and 602.14 still apply regardless. You cannot descend below the regulatory minimums in order to follow a voluntary noise abatement procedure. The regulations take precedence.

**Exam trap:** Noise abatement procedures = advisory/voluntary. CARs minimums = mandatory. Don't confuse them.

---

### Restricted Altitude Areas — Aerobatics

CARs 602.27 prohibits aerobatics:
- Over built-up areas
- Over an open-air assembly of persons
- In controlled airspace (without authorization)
- Below 1,000 feet AGL (generally)
- When the in-flight visibility is less than 3 miles

These restrictions overlap with the low-level flight rules because aerobatic manoeuvres at low altitude over populated areas combine two categories of risk.

---

### Summary Table

| Area | Minimum Altitude |
|------|-----------------|
| Built-up area (city/town) | 1,000 ft above highest obstacle within 2,000 ft |
| Open-air assembly of persons | 1,000 ft above highest obstacle within 2,000 ft |
| Open terrain (rural) | 602.13 principle; 500 ft AGL conventional minimum |
| Noise abatement procedures | Advisory only; CARs minimums still apply |

---

## Key Points

- **CARs 602.13:** No flight at altitude that prevents safe emergency landing — the underlying principle
- **CARs 602.14:** Over built-up areas and open-air assemblies — 1,000 feet above highest obstacle within 2,000 feet horizontally
- Over open/rural terrain: 500 feet AGL is the conventional minimum under the 602.13 principle
- Noise abatement procedures in the CFS are **voluntary** and advisory — not regulatory
- CARs minimums always apply; noise abatement procedures cannot override them
- Aerobatics prohibited over built-up areas and below 1,000 feet AGL under CARs 602.27
